    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Miramar

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Plumbing work in Florida follows the Florida Building Code, whose plumbing volume is based on the International Plumbing Code. An expansion tank gives that water somewhere to go, so the relief valve stops dripping and pipe joints stop taking pressure spikes every time the burner runs. We pre-charge it to your house pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Hybrid Heat Pump Water Heater Installation in Miramar, FL

    Heat pump water heaters pull warmth from the surrounding air and can use about a third of the power of a standard electric tank. They need roughly 700 cubic feet of air around them and a condensate drain, so a warm garage or a large utility room usually works better than a closet. Rebates may apply. We will tell you if your space works.

    Electric Tank Water Heater Installation in Miramar, FL

    Without gas service, an electric tank is the usual pick. We match voltage and breaker size to the panel, and since there is no vent, an electric tank can sit in spots a gas unit cannot. Closets and attics get a pan. Quiet and simple.

    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Miramar

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it. We size the tank by first-hour rating, not just gallons, because that number tells you how much hot water it delivers in a busy morning. Gas or electric both work. The fuel hookup, the venting and haul-away are all part of the job.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Miramar, FL

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. Heated water then has no way back to the street, so pressure rises and works on the relief valve, supply lines and toilet fill valves. An expansion tank absorbs it. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Gas Water Heater Installation in Miramar, FL

    Gas heaters recover faster than electric, often refilling a 50 gallon tank in under an hour. We install the gas line, the sediment trap and the vent connector, then check draft at the hood with a match or a smoke pencil to confirm exhaust is going up and out. Then we check for leaks. We add an expansion tank if code calls for it.

    Attic and Closet Water Heater Installs in Miramar

    Attic and closet installs take more planning than a garage swap. An attic heater needs a pan with its own drain line, a properly routed relief line and enough access to get the old tank out and the new one in. A leak lands on drywall. If moving it makes sense, we can relocate the heater.
