    Jammed Disposal Repair in Miami

    A humming disposal that will not spin usually has a jam. Bones, fruit pits, glass or utensils can wedge the flywheel. We cut power, use a hex wrench on the bottom of the unit to free the flywheel and remove the object. Then we test the motor and press the reset button if needed.

    Electrical Issues and Resetting in Miami

    If your garbage disposal isn't turning on, it could be an electrical problem or simply need a reset. Our technicians in Miami can safely diagnose electrical faults, check circuit breakers, and properly reset the unit. We also inspect wiring for damage or corrosion, which matters in a damp under-sink cabinet, making sure all electrical components are working correctly and safely.

    Slow Draining Disposal Repair in Miami, FL

    A disposal that grinds but drains slowly often has a clogged discharge tube or trap arm. Grease, rice and coffee grounds are the usual culprits. We clear the line with the right tools and check for proper slope. We also explain what not to put the disposal.

    Leaking Garbage Disposal Fix in Miami, FL

    Leaks from a garbage disposal can stem from various points, including loose sink flanges, worn-out seals, cracked housing, or pipe connections. We carefully inspect all potential leak sources, replacing faulty gaskets or tightening connections as needed. Addressing leaks promptly prevents water damage to your cabinets and flooring in Miami, FL.

    Disposal Reset and Electrical Repair in Miami

    When a disposal will not turn on at all, the thermal overload may have tripped or the switch may be bad. We check the reset button, breaker, wall switch and wiring. Air switches and batch-feed lids are tested too. Most electrical issues are easy to fix once found.

    Leaking Disposal Repair in Miami, FL

    Leaks can come from the sink flange, the dishwasher inlet, the discharge tube or the disposal body itself. We trace the leak, reseal the flange with plumber's putty, and replace gaskets or tubes as needed. A leak from the bottom of the unit usually means the internal seal has failed. In that case, replacement is the smarter option.
