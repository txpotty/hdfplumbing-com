    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Largo

    A standard flush and inspection typically runs $125 to $250 depending on tank size and access. Anode replacement typically adds $75 to $200 in parts and labor, and powered anodes cost more.

    That smell typically comes from bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. We can switch to an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank to clear it up.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which matters most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it extends the lifespan of your unit.

    Many manufacturers, including Rheem, A.O. Smith and Bradford White, expect regular maintenance and can deny claims for sediment damage. Keeping service records protects your warranty.

    In mineral-heavy water a magnesium rod may last three to five years. We measure it at every visit so you replace it before the tank lining starts to rust.

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving heavy scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. Mineral content contributes to sediment buildup in hot water tanks, which reduces efficiency and can damage heating elements or tank linings over time. Regular flushing is especially important in hard water areas.

    Common signs include reduced hot water supply, odd noises like rumbling or popping, rust-colored water, a sulfur or rotten egg smell, or water leaking from the tank. These indicate issues that maintenance can often resolve.
