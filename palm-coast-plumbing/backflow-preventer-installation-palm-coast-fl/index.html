    Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA) Installation in Palm Coast

    A double check valve assembly, or DCVA, uses two spring-loaded checks in series with four test cocks. It is the usual pick for low-hazard lines such as many fire sprinklers, and because it does not discharge water, it can go in a vault where local rules allow it, sized so it does not rob your system of pressure in Palm Coast. We leave room for the tester.

    Pool and Spa Fill Backflow Protection in Palm Coast, FL

    Pool auto-fill lines and hose connections give chlorinated water a straight path back to the supply. The fill line gets the correct backflow device, check that every hose bibb has a vacuum breaker, and can fold the work into a pool build or remodel so it is done before the deck goes in. Hose bibb breakers are cheap.

    Backflow Prevention for Irrigation Systems in Palm Coast, FL

    Sprinkler heads sit in fertilizer and standing water, and a pressure drop on the main can siphon that water back into the house. On most homes, a pressure vacuum breaker handles the back-siphonage risk, and we mount it at least 12 inches above the highest sprinkler head so it can do its job in Palm Coast, FL. Mounting height is the common mistake.

    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Palm Coast, FL

    Pressure vacuum breakers are the low-cost answer for lawn irrigation in Palm Coast, FL. They let air in when supply pressure drops, which breaks the siphon, but they only protect against back-siphonage, so they must sit above every downstream outlet and cannot be used where back pressure is possible. We check clearance and drainage too.

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Assembly Installation in Palm Coast

    Two independent checks and a relief valve make up an RPZ. When pressure between the checks gets too close to supply pressure, the relief opens, dumping water to the floor or a drain so nothing can pass upstream, which is why we never bury one in a pit in Palm Coast. We install Watts and Febco units.
