    Pop-Up Drain Replacement in Palm Coast

    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. The old body comes out, the seat gets cleaned, and the new flange goes in sealed with a clevis set for easy lifting. Finish matches the faucet. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    Bathroom Sink Drain Replacement in Palm Coast, FL

    Bathroom drains wear out at the pop-up. The pivot nut packing leaks, the drain body corrodes around the overflow holes, and the stopper stops sealing, so a sink that will not hold water usually needs a whole new pop-up assembly. Half-fixes do not hold here. The new drain matches your faucet finish.

    Kitchen Sink Drain Replacement in Palm Coast

    Kitchen drains start failing at the basket strainer. The putty under a 3-1/2 inch strainer dries out, the locknut rusts, and water seeps down the outside of the tailpiece where it looks like the trap is leaking even when it is not. Strainer and trap get swapped as a pair. Disposal sinks get a new flange instead.

    Leaking Drain Assembly Replacement in Palm Coast

    Water on the cabinet floor needs tracking, not guessing. After drying everything we fill and drain the bowl and watch each joint with a dry towel. Supply leaks look similar. We check those too.

    Bathroom Drain Upgrade in Palm Coast, FL

    Old bath drains often carry decades of hair and scum. Replacing the pop-up body and the trap removes the rough inside walls that grab hair, and a smooth new 1-1/4 inch path drains a full bowl in seconds instead of minutes. Repeat clogs drop off. A hair catcher keeps it that way.

    Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Palm Coast

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. Sinks below the sewer line drain through a pump, so we check its valve and discharge line during the job. Pump trouble is easy to miss. A gurgling pit is a clue.

    Push-Button Drain Installation in Palm Coast, FL

    Push-button drains get rid of the lift rod. They open and close with a press on the cap, and we check that the sink has an overflow before choosing a slotted body, since a slotted drain in a bowl without an overflow leaks out the slots. They clean easier too. Hair still collects, so pull the cap now and then.
