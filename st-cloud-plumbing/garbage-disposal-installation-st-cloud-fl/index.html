    Disposal Installation in St. Cloud, FL: How We Do It

    Disposal Rough-In for New Construction in St. Cloud

    At rough-in we set the drain height and the outlet location before the cabinets go in.     Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in St. Cloud

    New disposals come with a knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. Forgetting the plug floods the dishwasher. We also install an air gap or high loop, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher while the disposal is draining.

    Replacing an Old Disposal in St. Cloud, FL

    Old disposals usually come off with a twist of the mounting ring, once the power is off and the dishwasher hose and discharge tube are disconnected. If the new unit is the same brand and mount style, the swap is faster, but we still replace the flange gasket and putty when the old seal has hardened. Old gaskets go stiff. The old disposal leaves with us.

    High-Capacity Disposal Installation in St. Cloud, FL

    Busy kitchens get the most from a 1 HP unit, like an InSinkErator Evolution or a Waste King Legend model. We confirm the circuit can carry the motor and the drain can handle the flow, because a strong unit pushing into a flat trap arm will still clog. These units weigh more. They get supported during mounting.

    Standard Disposal Installation in St. Cloud

    Most households in St. Cloud do well with a 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower continuous-feed unit. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Wiring and testing come last. We listen for a smooth spin and watch the drain clear.

    Commercial Disposal Setup in St. Cloud

    Restaurants and commercial kitchens in St. Cloud need disposals built to run all day.     Batch-Feed Disposal Installation in St. Cloud, FL

    A batch-feed disposal will not start until the lid is in, and the lid holds a magnet that closes a switch inside the unit. No wall switch is needed, which makes them handy in kitchens where running a new switch would mean opening finished walls. They are slower to use. Families with small kids often like the trade.

    Batch-Feed Disposal Setup in St. Cloud
