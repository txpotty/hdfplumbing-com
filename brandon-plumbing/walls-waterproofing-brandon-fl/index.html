    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Brandon

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. A high water table across much of Florida affects drainage, septic design, and how deeply crews can dig for underground repairs. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Brandon

    Newer homes across Florida mostly use PEX or CPVC with PVC drains, though copper pinhole leaks are still a frequent repair in older homes. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with steady hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Some basements never need a sump.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Brandon, FL

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Brandon

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Constant heat and humidity are the main plumbing stress in Florida, keeping water heaters, AC drain lines, and outdoor pipes working year round. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Brandon, FL

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Brandon

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system usually runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, with the new tile priced separately. Block wall sealing outside often costs $800 to $3,000. Foundation waterproofing below grade is usually $2,500 to $8,000.

    Xypex, Tremco, Sika and Henry products show up on a lot of our jobs, covering crystalline products, elastomeric membranes and sealants. The product depends on the wall. Crystalline coatings work well on poured concrete, but block walls usually call for an elastomeric membrane or drainage board.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Larger foundation jobs can run several days, and weather can stretch that.

    It depends on the wall and the site. On exterior foundation work we use liquid polyurethane-modified asphalt, rubberized asphalt sheets, bentonite panels and crystalline coatings, picking the one that suits the soil and the wall material. For showers, we use Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban.

    Look low on the wall first. Damp patches, a musty smell, mold on the bottom of the drywall, a white powder on block or brick called efflorescence, and puddles after rain all point to water coming through. Stair-step cracks and bowing walls also deserve a closer look.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on weather and how deep the footing sits. We give you a timeline at the first visit.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, and the framing rots faster than it would have if you had left it open. We repair the leak and dry the cavity first.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to the method we use, the quality of the install and the soil conditions around your house. Sump pumps wear out sooner. Plan on a new pump every seven to ten years.
