    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Sarasota

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. After about ten years, plan on replacing it.

    A straight pump replacement generally goes without a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we check the local rules and pull permits when they apply.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Parts for all four are easy to find at supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. We stock the common models.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. A tune-up covers the float, the pit, the check valve and the discharge, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. It takes under an hour.

    Labor on our sump pump installs is warrantied, typically for one year. Each pump also has a factory warranty, usually one to five years depending on the brand and model, and we write the exact coverage on your invoice so you know which part to call about if something fails. Save the paperwork.
