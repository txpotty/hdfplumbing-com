    Submersible Well Pump Repair in University

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in University

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. Shaft seal leaks and worn impellers come next. The pump gets reprimed and resealed, and the foot valve and suction line get checked, because a single air leak will undo the work. Everything is above ground.

    Jet Pump System Repair in University, FL

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. HDF Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Low Water Pressure Diagnosis in University

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. In some cases the pump is fine and the well just cannot keep up with demand. We test each one.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in University

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. After pulling the pump, we test each part and replace only the failed one. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your University, FL well.
