    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Palm Harbor

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. Nights and weekends run higher.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. Both brands hold up to daily use. We keep cables and cutter heads in several sizes on board, which lets the tech match the tool to your pipe instead of forcing one setup on every drain.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water rising in a tub or shower when another fixture drains is a strong sign of a main line clog. Act on that quickly. The longer it goes, the more likely the next flush ends with sewage on the bathroom floor.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. It works well on hair, wipes and roots. Hydro jetting washes the entire inside of the pipe with high-pressure water, removing grease, scale and sludge the cable leaves on the walls. For recurring clogs, jetting is the better bet.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. Main lines with heavy roots, or jobs that need a jetting pass, can stretch to half a day or more. Access makes the biggest difference. A buried or missing cleanout means pulling a toilet first, which adds about an hour.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera look can set the timing. Seeing how fast roots came back since the last cutting tells us whether to plan a yearly visit or stretch it to two years.

    We run RIDGID, Spartan and Gorlitz augers, plus sewer cameras and jetters from the same tier of commercial makers. These machines take daily abuse and keep working. Brand matters less than setup. Even a top machine can hurt a pipe with the wrong head, so picking the cutter gets as much attention as the machine.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots chase that moisture to the joint and work their way inside. Once in, they grow quickly. In Palm Harbor, clay with joints every couple of feet and old cast iron with lead and oakum joints get hit the most.
