    Grease Trap Pumping and Hauling in Gainesville

    Trap waste is regulated as a liquid waste, so in most places it has to be hauled by a licensed hauler to an approved receiving facility, not dumped down a manhole or into a dumpster.     Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Gainesville, FL

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. We open every compartment and pump each one to the floor.     Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. It pours as a liquid and sets up hard once it cools. In a sewer pipe it sticks to the walls in layers, catches food scraps and wipes, and eventually closes the pipe down to a pencil-sized opening.

    That is usually hydrogen sulfide from grease breaking down in an overdue trap. A worn lid gasket makes it worse. Pumping the trap, scraping the walls and replacing a flattened gasket fixes most cases, and if it comes back fast we check the vent and the floor drain traps nearby for dry seals.

    Watch the floor sinks during dish rush. Water that lingers in them is an early warning. Other signs include a rancid smell near the dish station, grease showing up in the cleanout nearest the street, and drains that gurgle after the dish machine empties.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. The disposal is the part that gets people fined. Many programs want a licensed hauler's record for every cleaning, and a kitchen doing its own has nothing to show the inspector.
