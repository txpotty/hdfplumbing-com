    Water Pressure Restoration in Gainesville

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. We put a gauge on a hose bib first.     Main Water Line Repair in Gainesville

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Gainesville

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. The usual code limit is 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, then swap a failed regulator for a new Watts or Zurn Wilkins valve when it reads too high. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. So the shutoff works when you need it.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Gainesville

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Houses from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s may have polybutylene supply pipe, which many insurers refuse to cover and owners choose to replace. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or abandon the old pipe under the floor and run new PEX above it. We price each one for you.
