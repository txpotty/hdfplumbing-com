    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Tampa, FL

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Tampa, FL. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED.     Propane Water Heater Repair in Tampa

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Tampa

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Tampa home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Tampa, FL

    Restaurants and salons in Tampa, FL burn through hot water faster than any house, and a down heater stops the business.     Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Tampa

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Tampa, FL

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. The tech reads the code and tests the thermopile and sensors before condemning the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. We leave the old valve with you if you ask.
