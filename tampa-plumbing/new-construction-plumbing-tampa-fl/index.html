    Guest House, ADU and Addition Plumbing in Tampa, FL

    Guest houses and additions have to tie into the existing house without overloading old drain lines. Before tying in, we run a camera down the existing sewer.     Underground and Under-Slab Plumbing in Tampa

    Underground work sets the drain, waste and vent layout that every fixture in the house relies on.     Picking the water heater during design saves a gas line reroute later. A 50-gallon tank covers most three-bedroom homes. Tankless units suit bigger houses but need a larger gas line and their own vent route, both planned at rough-in. We set expansion tanks and pans where code calls for them.

    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Tampa

    Most new single-family homes in the Tampa area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Gas piping and tankless heaters push it up. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Fixtures carry their own manufacturer warranties. Water heaters and PEX systems often carry 10 years or more, but only when installed with the matching tools and fittings, which is one reason we do not mix brands.

    Salt air in coastal parts of Florida corrodes outdoor fittings and exterior metal pipe faster than in inland areas. When a rare freeze is forecast, covering outdoor valves and letting a faucet drip is usually enough to prevent damage. We pick pipe routes and insulation to match. That can mean keeping supply lines out of exterior walls, insulating attic runs, and setting frost-free hose bibs that drain back inside the heated space. Attics get the most attention.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine. We ask that they arrive on site before trim day so we can open the boxes and check for missing parts, because a missing cartridge or wrong finish can push trim back a week.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drains are PVC or ABS. Some builders want cast iron in walls beside bedrooms, since it is far quieter than PVC when an upstairs toilet flushes.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. The underground inspection happens before the slab is poured, with drains under a water or air test. Top-out gets inspected before insulation, and the final comes after fixtures are set and running. Some add a gas test.

    Yes, softeners are easy to plan in. Many wells in Florida produce water with a sulfur smell or high iron, which often calls for filtration to protect fixtures and appliances. The loop includes a bypass and a drain, and we usually keep the kitchen cold line and hose bibs on unsoftened water. A loop at rough-in is cheap.

    Plumbing is usually covered under the general building permit. If the jurisdiction wants a separate plumbing permit, our license goes on it. Either way, we schedule the plumbing inspections, attend them, and handle any corrections without the builder having to chase us down about it.
