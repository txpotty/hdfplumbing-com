    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Sunrise

    The cost of water heater installation in Sunrise, FL can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any needed plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units usually have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your specific needs.

    A traditional tank water heater typically lasts between 8 and 12 years with proper maintenance. Tankless water heaters often have a longer lifespan, ranging from 15 to 20 years, thanks to their durable components and on-demand operation.

    Most households of two to four people do well with a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by gallons per minute.

    An expansion tank absorbs excess water pressure created as water heats up inside the tank. Many homes in Sunrise, FL, particularly those with closed-loop plumbing systems, are required by code to have an expansion tank to protect the water heater and plumbing system from damage.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, water heater installation involves working with gas lines, high voltage electricity and water connections, all of which carry significant risks if not handled correctly. Improper installation can lead to leaks, gas explosions, electrical fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. It can also void your warranty, and local codes often require a licensed plumber for this work.

    We install Rheem, A.O. Smith, Bradford White, Navien, Rinnai and other trusted brands. We recommend models based on reliability, warranty and budget.

    Many tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, though hard water can shorten that. Regular flushing and anode rod checks help extend life.

    To improve efficiency, ensure your new water heater is properly sized for your home, consider a tankless model for on-demand heating, insulate hot water pipes and set the thermostat to 120°F (49°C) to prevent scalding and cut energy consumption.
