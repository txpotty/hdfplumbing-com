    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Sanford

    Expect roughly $1,500 to $4,000 for a small indoor trap and $7,000 to $20,000 or more for an outdoor gravity interceptor. Recessing an indoor unit into the slab pushes the price up because of saw-cutting and patching. Location drives the rest. Lids under traffic cost more, and so does repaving.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. Our labor warranty covers the joints and the setting, and you get it in writing up front.

    Plan on a permit. Both the building department and the sewer authority usually have a say. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We add up the drain rate of each sink compartment, floor sink and dish machine, then apply the code's drain-down time to get gallons per minute. Gravity tanks use volume instead. Outdoor tanks get sized by the sewer authority's formula, which we run for you.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. Grease odor in the dining room is another. Rust shows up too. On steel traps we look for pinholes at the seams, and on any trap we check whether the outlet line downstream is coated with grease that should have been caught.

    As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap. Indoor units need room above for the lid to come off and for someone to kneel and clean. Never under a cook line. Outdoor tanks should sit where the hauler can reach them and nothing gets parked on the lids.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. We register the unit with the maker so the factory warranty is on record. Keep your cleaning logs. Makers may ask for service records before paying a claim.
