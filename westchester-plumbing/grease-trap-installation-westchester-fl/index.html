    Small indoor hydromechanical traps usually cost $1,500 to $5,000 installed. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more with excavation, concrete and site restoration. We provide a written estimate after a site visit.

    Grease trap installation is a complex commercial plumbing task that requires specialized knowledge, tools, and adherence to strict local codes. Attempting a DIY installation can lead to costly errors, code violations, and potential health hazards. It is always best to hire licensed and qualified plumbers like HDF Plumbing.

    Indoor traps should be close to the fixtures they serve and easy to open for cleaning. Outdoor interceptors are usually placed where a pump truck can reach them without blocking customer traffic.

    We calculate flow from every connected fixture, including sinks, floor sinks and dish machines. Hydromechanical units are usually rated by gallons per minute, while gravity interceptors are sized by volume and retention time.

    The primary purpose of a grease trap is to intercept fats, oils, and greases (FOG) from commercial kitchen wastewater before they enter the public sewer system. This prevents pipe blockages, protects municipal infrastructure, and helps businesses comply with environmental regulations.

    We recommend and install high-quality, durable grease trap brands such as Zurn, Big Dipper, Schier, and Rockford Separators. These manufacturers are known for their reliability and compliance with industry standards, offering various solutions to meet different commercial kitchen needs.

    Installation time depends on the size and complexity of the grease trap.     The cleaning frequency for a new grease trap depends on its size, the volume of FOG produced by your kitchen, and local regulations in Westchester. Typically, grease traps require cleaning every 1 to 3 months. Regular cleaning is essential to maintain efficiency and prevent backups.
