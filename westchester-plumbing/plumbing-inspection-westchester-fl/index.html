    Residential Plumbing Inspections in Westchester

    A full home inspection covers the system from the water meter to the sewer connection. Our plumber looks at all exposed pipe, runs every faucet and toilet and tests the heater, the main valve and the fixture stops. Pressure gets measured too. High pressure gets flagged.

    Water Heater and Softener Inspection in Westchester

    We check the water heater's age by serial number, look for rust, leaks and sediment and test the relief valve. We confirm proper venting. We also look at the expansion tank, drain pan and softener, and a hardness test shows whether the softener is working or just eating salt.

    Preventive Maintenance Checks in Westchester, FL

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems. We flush the water heater, exercise the main shutoff and angle stops, check pressure and look for slow drains and weeping fittings. It takes about an hour. Doing this every year or two adds years to a water heater and keeps the shutoffs working for the day you really need them.

    Leak Detection and Assessment in Westchester, FL

    Hidden leaks waste water and quietly damage floors and walls. Start at the meter. We shut everything off, check the meter's leak dial and then follow the leak with acoustic equipment and thermal imaging. Catching it early keeps it small.     Water Heater Inspections in Westchester

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Westchester

    A standard plumbing inspection in Westchester, FL usually costs $150 to $350. Size changes the price. That fee covers the supply pipe, fixtures, water heater, drains and pressure check, while a sewer camera run is usually priced as an add-on because it takes extra time and equipment. You get the price when you book.

    Watching your water meter, looking under sinks and checking for leaks are all things you can do yourself. A plumber adds video, pressure tests and an expert read.

    We identify supply pipe materials including polybutylene, Kitec, galvanized, copper, CPVC and PEX. Known risks and options go in the report.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It has limits. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Camera work takes longer. Larger homes, long sewer lines or a hidden leak that needs tracing can push it longer, and we will tell you the expected time when you book so you can plan around it.

    Most homes do well with an inspection every one to two years. Buying a home is another time. Buyers, owners of older houses and anyone with recent leaks or backups benefit from yearly checks that catch problems sooner.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. Damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell under a sink or a warm patch of tile can all point to a hidden leak that an inspection will find before it does more damage.
