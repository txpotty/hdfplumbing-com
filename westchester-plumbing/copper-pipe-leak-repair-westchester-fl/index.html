    Preventive Copper Pipe Maintenance in Westchester, FL

    Copper gives warning signs before it leaks. They are easy to overlook. Green crust at fittings, a stain on a basement ceiling and high static pressure all point to trouble ahead, and a walkthrough of exposed copper in the basement, crawlspace or utility room catches many of them. A regulator often helps.

    Under Slab Copper Leak Repair in Westchester, FL

    Copper lines under a slab can wear through where they rub concrete or gravel. Warm floors are a clue. We isolate hot and cold lines with pressure tests and locate the leak with acoustic and thermal equipment, so you can pick between a targeted floor repair and a reroute overhead. Both options get a price.

    Solder Joint Leak Repair in Westchester, FL

    Older solder joints can fail from poor original work, thermal expansion or vibration. Crusty fittings are a clue. A leaking joint usually weeps for a while before it drips, and we clean, reflux and resolder the joint or replace the fitting with new pipe on each side when the old copper is too thin. Then we pressure test it.

    Underground Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Westchester

    Buried copper service lines can leak for months before anyone notices. The meter gives it away. Every repair gets a pressure test.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Repair in Westchester

    Pinholes form when the inner wall of copper pipe erodes or pits. Hot lines get it worst. They often appear in thin Type M pipe and on hot water lines where velocity and temperature are higher, so we cut out the affected section and replace it with Type L copper or a press fitting. More pinholes often follow.

    Copper Fitting and Valve Repair in Westchester

    Angle stops, shutoff valves and copper fittings under sinks and behind toilets can weep or seize. Minerals jam them. Hard water buildup inside a valve can keep it from closing when you need it most, and quarter-turn valves go in to replace the old multi-turn stops. We test each one.

    Burst Copper Pipe Repair in Westchester, FL

    Copper in an exterior wall or attic can split when the water inside freezes. Northern parts of Florida do get occasional freezing nights, which can crack exposed pipes, irrigation lines, and outdoor devices. Shut the main first. The split often does not leak until the ice thaws, so a pipe that froze overnight may start spraying the next afternoon once the house warms up. We cut out the split and solder in new pipe. Insulation goes on after.
