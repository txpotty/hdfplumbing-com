    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Wellington

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, while PEX gets a fresh crimp ring or expansion fitting with the right tool for the brand.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Wellington

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Wellington

    Underground leaks hide well. The first clue is often a water bill that jumped for no reason. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or a spot near the walk that stays muddy days after rain. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Wellington, FL

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. We shut it down first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Same-day repairs are the norm. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Main Water Line Repair in Wellington

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Wellington

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. Confirming it takes about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and continued movement tells us the leak is in the buried line. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Wellington, FL

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    Most jurisdictions in Florida require a permit for a full service line replacement. Small repairs depend on the city.     In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so we confirm where the leak sits before quoting anything. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, plus accidental hits from fence posts, irrigation trenching and utility work. Poor original installation is another, like rocks dumped right on the pipe.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Damage it causes may be. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, which can pay for the repair or replacement of the buried line itself. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Wellington, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.
