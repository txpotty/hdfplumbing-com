    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Wellington

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Wellington typically falls between $250 and $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. Extra work such as a rotted flange or soft subfloor raises the price, and we quote it before we begin.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. Expect a higher price and more weight. A two-piece bolts the tank to the bowl with a rubber gasket, which makes it lighter to carry up stairs and cheaper to fix, since you can replace just the tank or just the bowl if one cracks. Both flush well.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. A straight swap on a solid flange is a reasonable DIY job. New tile, a low or broken flange, a wall-hung carrier, or a toilet that has to move are the jobs where a plumber saves you money, because mistakes there end up under the floor.

    It is a toilet with the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly chair height, compared with 15 inches or so on a standard bowl. Most adults find it easier on the knees. Kids and shorter adults sometimes find their feet do not rest flat, so in a shared bathroom we sometimes suggest a standard bowl with a grab bar instead.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. Swapping in the same place usually does not. An inspector checks the new drain and vent while things are still open, and we book that visit around your remodel schedule.

    Two warranties apply. The toilet is covered by its maker. Parts usually get one to five years and the china often far longer, and our guarantee picks up any leak caused by the install.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.
