    Pressure Switch Replacement in Fort Myers, FL

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are common causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to suit your new tank's pre-charge.

    Bladder-Style Pressure Tank Replacement in Fort Myers, FL

    Most modern well pressure tanks are bladder-style, separating air and water for optimal efficiency. When the bladder fails, you'll experience symptoms like short-cycling of the well pump. HDF Plumbing carefully replaces these bladder-style tanks across Fort Myers, FL, ensuring proper pre-charge pressure and system integration for long-lasting performance.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Fort Myers

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and restrict flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives reliable readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in how well the system holds up.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Fort Myers

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A simple test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and verify the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.
