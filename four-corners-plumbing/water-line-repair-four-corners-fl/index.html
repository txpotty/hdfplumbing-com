    Main Water Line Repair in Four Corners

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Four Corners

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. Confirming it takes about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and continued movement tells us the leak is in the buried line. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Four Corners

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. So the shutoff works when you need it.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Four Corners

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, and we check the exposed pipe on both sides for green crust or soft spots.
