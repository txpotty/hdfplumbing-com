    What a Water Heater Tune-Up Covers in Kendall, FL

    Tankless Descaling in Kendall, FL

    Scale is the tankless unit's main enemy. Many wells in Florida produce water with a sulfur smell or high iron, which often calls for filtration to protect fixtures and appliances. We close the isolation valves, connect a small pump and circulate descaling solution through the heat exchanger for the time the manufacturer recommends, then flush it with clean water. The inlet screen gets cleaned too. Igniter, burner and venting get a safety check.

    Anode Rod Checks in Kendall

    The anode rod rusts so the tank does not. It is a long metal rod screwed into the top of the tank, and once it has corroded down to bare wire, the water starts eating the steel tank lining instead. We pull it and look. Replacing the rod is much cheaper than replacing the heater.

    Flushing a Tank Water Heater in Kendall

    Popping and rumbling mean sediment. Calcium and magnesium settle on the bottom of the tank and form a layer that traps heat under it, so water boils in pockets beneath the scale and pops every time the burner or lower element fires. We flush until it runs clear. Bad tanks need a few rounds.

    Yearly Service Plans in Kendall, FL

    Forgetting is the main reason tanks go unflushed. A yearly plan puts the visit on the calendar, sends a reminder a week ahead, and gives you priority scheduling and repair discounts if the heater ever acts up between visits. We track it for you. Long-term savings add up.

    Tank Flush and Inspection in Kendall

    Sediment forms from minerals in the water. We flush the tank, test the relief valve, check the anode and look over each fitting. The tank heats better. So does recovery time.

    Water Heater Problem Diagnosis in Kendall, FL

    Something sounds or feels wrong. Rumbling, short hot water or a wet pan get diagnosed on the spot, with a straight answer on repair or replace. We lay out choices. No pressure either way.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Kendall

    Some water eats anode rods fast. Depending on your water chemistry, a rod that should last five years might be spent in three, so we pull it on every visit instead of assuming it is fine because the tank is still young. We swap worn ones. A healthy rod keeps the tank from rusting through.

    Descaling Tankless Units in Kendall

    Error codes often mean scale. Tankless heaters are especially sensitive to hard water, and once scale coats the heat exchanger, flow drops, the unit overheats and the control board shuts it down to protect itself. A pump kit handles it. Yearly descaling is standard advice for hard water.
