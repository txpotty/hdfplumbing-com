    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in Kendall

    The cost of gas line installation in Kendall, FL varies a lot depending on the length of the line, the piping material, routing complexity and the number of appliances. A simple appliance hookup might range from $400 to $800, while a whole-home installation could be several thousand dollars. We provide a clear estimate after an on-site assessment.

    The most common sign of a gas leak is the smell of rotten eggs, which comes from an additive called mercaptan. Other signs include a hissing sound near a gas line, dead vegetation over an underground gas line or an unexplained increase in your gas bill. If you suspect a leak, evacuate immediately and call 911 and your gas company.

    Yes, a permit is almost always required for any new gas line installation or significant change to an existing gas system. This confirms the work is performed to code and inspected for safety. We handle the permitting process in Kendall on your behalf to keep the project in compliance.

    Yes, we can install the gas line needed to switch from an electric appliance, such as a water heater or clothes dryer, to a gas-powered one. This often involves extending an existing gas line or installing a new one, along with proper venting if needed. We ensure all connections are safe and meet appliance specifications.

    We primarily install black iron pipe and corrugated stainless steel tubing, or CSST. Black iron is a traditional, rigid option, while CSST offers flexibility and faster installation. Both are safe and code-compliant when installed by licensed professionals, and the choice often depends on the application and property layout.

    Most residential gas line installations for a single appliance can be completed in a few hours to one day. More complex projects, such as extending lines for outdoor kitchens or multiple appliances, might take one to two days. This timeline does not include permitting and inspections, which can add several days to the schedule.

    CSST bonding connects the gas piping to the home's electrical grounding system to reduce damage from lightning strikes. It is a code and manufacturer requirement we follow on every install.
