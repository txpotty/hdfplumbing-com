    Deep Well Pump Repair in Clearwater

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up, run quietly and last, but motors, impellers and splices still wear out.     Well Pressure Tank Repair in Clearwater

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. When the bladder fails, the tank fills with water and the pump starts and stops every time someone opens a faucet. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. A properly sized tank can add years to pump life.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Service in Clearwater, FL

    Franklin Electric motors and control boxes are in a lot of local wells. Our technicians know them well, from the QD control boxes to the SubDrive constant pressure units. We carry the common Franklin parts. A motor that tests bad on a megohm meter usually gets replaced rather than rewound, since a new motor costs less than shipping and rebuilding the old one.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Clearwater, FL

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. After a major storm, homes on wells or septic should be checked before normal use, since floodwater can foul both systems. Surges from lightning are the usual cause of control box failures. We test capacitors, relays and overload protection, replace what failed and recommend a surge arrestor at the box if one is missing. Arrestors are cheap.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Clearwater, FL

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. HDF Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and confirms it builds pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.
