    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Kendale Lakes

    Expect roughly $350 to $800 for a standard, chair height or high-efficiency toilet installed, with the old one removed and hauled away. A rusted flange or a cramped bathroom can raise it. We give you a firm number before we start.

    Replacement wins when repair costs start to approach the price of a new toilet, or when the toilet is old enough to waste water every time it flushes. Cracks settle it. Frequent clogs and a rocking base point the same way.

    Handy homeowners can do it, but the trouble starts when the old toilet comes off and the flange turns out to be cracked or sitting below the new floor. Most DIY leaks start there. Overtightened closet bolts are the other common mistake, and that one cracks a new bowl.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drain, which you can find by measuring to the closet bolts. Twelve inches is standard. Some older houses have a 10 or 14 inch rough-in instead, and a toilet made for the wrong one either sticks out or will not fit.

    Watch for cracks, weekly clogs, a base that rocks after it has been reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. Age matters too. Toilets past 25 years old are often worth replacing even if nothing is broken yet.

    A pro install costs more up front, but it comes with a flange check, the right closet bolts and a warranty if the seal leaks later. A bad DIY seal can rot a subfloor. Fixing that costs much more than paying for the install.
