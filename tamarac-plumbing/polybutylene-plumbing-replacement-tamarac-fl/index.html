    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Tamarac

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper runs higher, and a yard line usually adds $1,500 to $4,000.

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving heavy scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. It adds scale. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. Copper replacements use Type L instead of thin Type M, since the heavier wall lasts longer.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. Manufacturer coverage adds more. You get both warranties in writing at the end of the job, along with photos and the permit record, so everything is in one folder if you ever need it.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper often goes further. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.
