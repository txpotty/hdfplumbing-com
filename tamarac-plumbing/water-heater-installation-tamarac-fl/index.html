    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Tamarac

    The cost of water heater installation in Tamarac, FL can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any needed plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units generally have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a written, upfront quote after assessing your specific needs.

    Rusty water, leaks around the base, rumbling noises and running out of hot water quickly are common signs. If your heater is over 10 years old, replacement is often the smart move.

    Manufacturer warranties usually range from 6 to 12 years on the tank and parts. We also warranty our installation labor.

    The typical lifespan of a traditional tank water heater is about 10 to 15 years, while tankless water heaters can last 20 years or more with proper maintenance. Factors like local water quality, maintenance frequency and usage habits can all influence the actual lifespan of your unit. Regular flushing can help extend its life.

    The correct size water heater depends on the number of people in your household and your peak hot water demand. For tank units, a 40 to 50 gallon tank is common for 2 to 4 people, while larger families might need 60 to 80 gallons. For tankless units, it is about flow rate (GPM) and temperature rise. Our plumbers will assess your needs to recommend the right size.

    A standard tank replacement typically takes two to four hours. Tankless installs or relocations can take most of a day.

    A traditional tank water heater typically lasts between 8 and 12 years with proper maintenance. Tankless water heaters often have a longer lifespan, ranging from 15 to 20 years, thanks to their durable components and on-demand operation.

    In most areas, a plumbing permit is required for water heater replacement or installation to ensure the work meets local building and safety codes. Plumbing work in Florida follows the Florida Building Code, whose plumbing volume is based on the International Plumbing Code.
