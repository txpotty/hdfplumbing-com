    Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium deposits on flush valve seats, fill valve ports and rim jets. Over time this causes leaks, slow refills and weak flushes. Descaling and choosing parts made to resist scale help repairs last longer.

    If the porcelain is sound and the toilet flushes well, a repair is generally the smart choice. If it uses 3.5 gallons or more per flush, clogs often or has a cracked tank, replacing it with a 1.28 gallon WaterSense model often makes more sense.

    The most common causes are a worn flapper, a mineral-crusted flush valve seat or a fill valve set too high so water spills into the overflow tube. A chain that is too short can also hold the flapper open. A quick inspection typically pinpoints the cause in minutes.

    Most standard toilet repairs, such as replacing a flapper, fill valve, or wax ring, can be completed within one to two hours by an skilled plumber. More complex issues like clearing a deep clog or diagnosing intermittent problems might take a bit longer, but we strive for efficiency.

    Common signs include a toilet that runs nonstop, a weak or incomplete flush, water leaking around the base, a rocking or unstable bowl, or a visible crack in the porcelain. Any unusual noises, like gurgling or whistling, can also indicate an internal issue that needs attention.
