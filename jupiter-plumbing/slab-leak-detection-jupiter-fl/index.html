    How We Find Slab Leaks in Jupiter, FL

    Slab Leaks in Older Homes in Jupiter, FL

    Older homes in Jupiter, FL often have soft copper or galvanized steel under the slab, and both corrode with age. Copper fails at bends and where it rubs concrete, while galvanized rusts from the inside until it splits. Our techs know where these fail. It lets us test the most likely spots first.

    Tracer Gas Leak Detection in Jupiter

    PEX and CPVC do not carry sound well. For those, we drain the line and push in a safe hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas, which rises through the slab and gets picked up by a sniffer at the floor surface, often through grout joints and hairline cracks. It catches leaks the microphone cannot in Jupiter homes.

    Thermal Imaging for Slab Leaks in Jupiter, FL

    Hot water leaking under the slab warms the floor above it. A thermal camera shows that warm bloom through tile, vinyl and even thin carpet, often spreading outward from the leak point in a recognizable shape. It only works on hot lines. We use it to narrow the search before the acoustic check.

    Commercial Slab Leak Detection in Jupiter, FL

    Offices, shops and restaurants on slabs in Jupiter, FL can lose a lot of water fast because their supply lines are larger. We test after hours if needed. HDF Plumbing isolates zones, traces mains and branch lines under the floor, then marks the leak so the repair fits around your hours.

    Pressure Testing and Isolation in Jupiter

    We always start with isolation. Our tech closes the main, caps or valves off the hot and cold sides, and puts a gauge on each, then watches which one drops over fifteen to thirty minutes. The gauge points us to the right line. It also rules out a toilet or faucet leak masquerading as a slab leak in your Jupiter, FL home.

    Repiping After Repeat Slab Leaks in Jupiter, FL

    Two slab leaks usually means more are coming. The rest of the pipe has likely aged the same way, so repiping replaces every supply line with new PEX or copper through the walls and attic, ending the cycle of floor openings and surprise water bills. A full repipe takes about two to four days.
