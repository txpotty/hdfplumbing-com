    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Boynton Beach, FL

    The anode rod, often called the sacrificial rod, is vital for preventing corrosion inside your hot water tank. Water chemistry varies from one neighborhood to the next, and it affects the rod's lifespan. Our technicians inspect the anode rod for wear and recommend replacement if it's significantly corroded, typically every 3 to 5 years. Replacing a worn anode rod is a cost-effective way to protect your tank from rust and leaks, saving you from a full tank replacement.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Boynton Beach

    The temperature and pressure relief valve is the part that keeps a tank from becoming dangerous. We lift the lever, confirm it opens and reseats and check the discharge line for proper material and termination. We also check your thermal expansion tank pressure with a gauge, since a waterlogged expansion tank lets pressure spike every heating cycle. High street pressure is not unusual in many service areas, so we test static pressure at a hose bib too.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Boynton Beach

    The cost of hot water tank maintenance in Boynton Beach, FL typically ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the tank type, age and specific services required. This normally includes flushing, anode rod inspection and safety valve checks. We provide upfront pricing before any work begins.

    Once a year is the baseline for most homes, and more often where water is hard. Homes without a softener or with heavy use may do better with a flush every six months.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend yearly hot water tank maintenance. This is particularly true where water is hard, since minerals speed up sediment buildup and anode rod depletion.

    Many manufacturers, including Rheem, A.O. Smith and Bradford White, expect regular maintenance and can reject claims for sediment damage. Keeping service records safeguards your warranty.

    Most flushes and inspections take 45 minutes to 90 minutes. Heavily scaled tanks or anode replacements in tight closets can take a bit longer.

    Many homeowners can do a basic drain with a garden hose. The risk is a plastic drain valve that breaks or will not reseal, and DIY flushes rarely include an anode check or relief valve test.
