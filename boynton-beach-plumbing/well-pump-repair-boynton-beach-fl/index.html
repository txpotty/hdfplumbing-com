    Well pump failures can be caused by several factors such as electrical problems (power surges, faulty wiring), mechanical wear and tear on pump components, sediment buildup in the well, or issues with the pressure tank or switch. Age and consistent use also contribute.

    Regular maintenance is essential. This includes annual inspections, checking the pressure tank's air charge, ensuring proper well cap sealing, and being mindful of your water usage. Avoiding continuous short cycling of the pump can also lengthen its life.

    The duration of a well pump repair depends on the issue. Simple fixes like a pressure switch replacement might take a few hours, while replacing a submersible pump can take a full day, especially if the well is deep. We strive for efficient service to restore your water as quickly as possible.

    Watch for sputtering faucets, low or fluctuating pressure, a pump that runs constantly, air in the lines or sudden spikes in your electric bill. A rapidly clicking pressure switch also signals trouble.

    It's generally recommended to have your well pump system inspected annually by a qualified plumber. Regular inspections can identify potential issues early, preventing major breakdowns and lengthening the lifespan of your well pump and associated components.
