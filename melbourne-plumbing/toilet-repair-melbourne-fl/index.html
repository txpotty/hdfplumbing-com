    Cracked Tank and Supply Line Repair in Melbourne, FL

    A hairline crack in the tank or a bulging braided supply line can let go without warning and flood a bathroom. We inspect the tank, tank-to-bowl bolts and gasket, and the supply line during every visit.     Toilet Gasket and Seal Replacement in Melbourne, FL

    The gaskets and seals within your toilet tank and at its base are crucial for preventing leaks. Over time, these rubber components can degrade, crack, or become brittle due to age and exposure to water, especially where the water is hard. We specialize in replacing worn-out flapper seals, tank-to-bowl gaskets, and the wax ring at the toilet's base, which is essential for preventing leaks onto your bathroom floor and maintaining a watertight seal.

    Clogged Toilet Clearing in Melbourne, FL

    Tough toilet clogs are a frequent problem that can bring your bathroom to a halt. While some can be cleared with a plunger, deeper blockages may point to issues further down the drain line or even in the main sewer line. Our technicians use professional-grade augers and sometimes even camera inspections to locate and remove obstructions effectively. We handle everything from foreign objects to excessive toilet paper, making sure your toilet drains freely again.

    Pressure-Assist and Dual-Flush Repair in Melbourne

    Pressure-assist toilets from Sloan Flushmate and dual-flush models from TOTO and American Standard use parts that hardware stores seldom stock. We carry cartridges, flush buttons and seals for the common models and can order specialty parts fast. These systems need correct pressure settings to work well, so we test incoming water pressure as part of the repair.

    Fill Valve Replacement in Melbourne

    A noisy, slow or hissing tank often points to a worn fill valve. Mineral scale clogs the small ports inside, which causes long refill times and phantom refills in the night. We replace old ballcock valves with modern adjustable fill valves from Fluidmaster or Korky and set the water level to the manufacturer mark. The job takes about 20 minutes and makes an older toilet noticeably quieter.
