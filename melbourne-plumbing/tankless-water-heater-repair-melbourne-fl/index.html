    What Our Tankless Plumbers Fix in Melbourne, FL

    Electric Tankless Element Repair in Melbourne, FL

    Electric units fail in their own way. Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart models use two to four heating elements in separate chambers, so a single burned element leaves the heater behind at high flow, while a failed flow switch means no heat at all. We check each element's resistance, measure voltage under load and check amperage on each leg, then confirm the panel and wire size match the unit's rating.

    Igniter and Flame Rod Repair in Melbourne, FL

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. An oxidized flame rod gives a weak microamp reading and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, look for a cracked igniter or a lazy spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Melbourne

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just when it first fires. We put a manometer on the inlet test port and run every hot tap in the house, since pressure that sags under load leads to flame loss and slugs of cold water. Then we check the exhaust. Codes come off when the numbers are right.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Melbourne, FL

    Rinnai units are common, and scale causes most of their service calls. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush. We also see dirty flame rods behind a code 11 or 12 and tired combustion fans behind a code 61, and we test every one against Rinnai's published values. Parts come from the factory list.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Melbourne

    Navien condensing units run a second stainless exchanger that pulls heat from the exhaust. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. On NPE-A models we also test the built-in recirculation pump, after clearing the trap and checking vent pitch. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Melbourne, FL

    Condensing tankless heaters recover heat from the flue gas and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. A plugged trap will lock the heater out. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Recirculation Pump Repair in Melbourne

    Recirculation cuts the wait for hot water at a far bathroom from a minute or more to a few seconds. When the pump fails or the check valve sticks, you get long waits again or lukewarm water in the cold line. We test the pump, check for a crossover valve under the far sink, and set the schedule so it runs around your mornings and evenings. Two settings, mostly.

    Navien Condensate & Fan Repair in Melbourne

    Navien's E016 is a heat exchanger overheat, and it usually traces to scale or a backed-up condensate line. We also read fan speed from the service menu and compare it to spec, since a fan that cannot reach target RPM will cause flame and exhaust faults on the same unit. We clean the trap and screen, then retest.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Melbourne

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350. Part repairs mostly fall between $175 and $600, and a heat exchanger replacement can top $800 when the warranty has run out. The quote comes before the wrench.

    Scale is first by a wide margin. After that come dirty flame rods, failed flow sensors and blocked air intakes, with control boards and fan motors showing up less often, usually on units past ten years old. Most of it is preventable.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen two or three times a year. Putting a softener or scale cartridge upstream cuts scale buildup a great deal, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    Watch for hot water that takes longer to show up, a burner that cycles on and off, water around the base, or a code on the display. Small leaks get worse inside the cabinet.

    Most of our calls are Rinnai, Navien and Noritz, with Rheem, Takagi and Bosch close behind. We order factory parts for anything we do not stock.

    You can clean the inlet water filter and the air intake screen on most models with the manual open beside you. Gas valves, igniters and anything behind the sealed combustion cover belong to a licensed plumber, and DIY work there can void the manufacturer warranty. Leave the burner alone.

    Temperature swings, a unit that shuts off mid-shower and any code on the display are the usual ones. If you smell rotten eggs near a gas unit, get out and call the gas company.

    Most take one to three hours on site. A descale by itself runs about ninety minutes, while a part that has to be ordered, like a control board, usually means a second visit within a few business days. You will know before the tech leaves.
