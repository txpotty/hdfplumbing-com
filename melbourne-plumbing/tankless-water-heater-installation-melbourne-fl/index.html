    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Melbourne

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirc pump on a timer or sensor keeps the hot line primed, cutting the wait at the far sink from about a minute to a few seconds. Less water goes down the drain.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Melbourne, FL

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Melbourne, FL homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Melbourne, FL

    Replacing an old tankless unit tends to go faster than a conversion. The gas line is already sized and the vent path already exists, so in many homes it is a matter of matching the connections, checking the vent for damage and hanging the new unit. Vent sizes can change by brand. We check before ordering.

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Melbourne, FL

    Upfront, a tankless install in Melbourne, FL costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. The gap shrinks over time. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. Ask for both prices.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Melbourne

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and several models come with a recirculation pump built in. The built-in pump can move hot water toward a distant bath on a timer, cutting the wait at the tap without a separate pump. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC. Condensing units also need a condensate drain.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Melbourne

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job since the tank's location may not suit the new unit. Mounting near the gas meter on a garage or outside wall shortens the gas line and venting, though it can add distance to the bathrooms. We weigh both. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Melbourne

    Gas tankless units in Melbourne are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. Most homes need the line upsized from the meter, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or larger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Melbourne, FL

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. Venting changes too. Both are on the estimate.
