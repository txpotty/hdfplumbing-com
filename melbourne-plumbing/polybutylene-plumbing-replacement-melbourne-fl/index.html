    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Melbourne

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. Size, fixtures and attic or crawlspace access set most of the cost, and you get a written quote after we see the house.

    We provide a written workmanship warranty on the repipe, and pipe makers offer their own long-term warranties. You get the details before we start.

    Many wells in Florida produce water with a sulfur smell or high iron, which often calls for filtration to protect fixtures and appliances. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Hot lines and fittings usually go before the rest.

    Most full poly repipes take three to five days, including drywall patching. Larger homes take longer. Fixture count, attic access and pipe choice set the schedule, and you will usually have water each evening.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. PEX makers add their own. Both warranties come in writing with the photos and permit record, all in one place for later.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.
