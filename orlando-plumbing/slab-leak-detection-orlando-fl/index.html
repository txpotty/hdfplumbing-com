    Pressure Testing and Isolation in Orlando

    Pressure isolation is always the first step. Our tech closes the main, caps or valves off the hot and cold sides, and puts a gauge on each, then watches which one drops over fifteen to thirty minutes. That tells us where to look. It also rules out a toilet or faucet leak masquerading as a slab leak in your Orlando, FL home.

    Non-Invasive Slab Leak Locating in Orlando, FL

    We touch the floor last. Pressure tests, line tracing, thermal scans and acoustic listening all happen from the surface in your Orlando, FL home, so nothing gets drilled or cut during the search. Once two tools agree, we mark the spot and go over repairs, some of which avoid the slab entirely.

    Emergency Slab Leak Detection in Orlando

    Supply and Drain Leaks Under the Slab in Orlando, FL

    Supply leaks and drain leaks act differently. Supply leaks are pressurized, so they move the meter, warm the floor on the hot side and run whether or not anyone uses water. Drain leaks only leak when water goes down, and they show up as odors, slow drains or damp floors near bathrooms. We test both kinds in your Orlando, FL home.

    Hot Water Slab Leak Detection in Orlando

    The hot side fails more than the cold. Heat speeds up corrosion, and the pipe expands and contracts every time someone showers, rubbing against concrete and gravel until it wears through. Warm floors and a water heater that runs nonstop are the signs. Energy bills often rise.

    Thermal Imaging for Slab Leaks in Orlando, FL

    A leaking hot line heats the concrete over it. A thermal camera shows that warm bloom through tile, vinyl and even thin carpet, often spreading outward from the leak point in a recognizable shape. Cold-side leaks will not show. We use it to narrow the search before the acoustic check.

    Tracer Gas Leak Detection in Orlando

    PEX and CPVC do not carry sound well. For those, we drain the line and push in a safe hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas, which rises through the slab and gets picked up by a sniffer at the floor surface, often through grout joints and hairline cracks. It finds leaks acoustic gear misses in Orlando homes.
