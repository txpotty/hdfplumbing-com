    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Orlando

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Orlando

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. We confirm it in about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and if it keeps creeping we know the leak is between the meter and the house. Ground microphones find the spot. We cut in a repair coupling or new pipe and pressure test before backfill.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Orlando, FL

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Two or more leaks on galvanized is a replacement. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Orlando, FL

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Around Orlando, flushing the water heater yearly and descaling tankless units helps keep scale buildup under control. Scale builds up inside and the opening shrinks. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Orlando

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the house shutoff and the meter-side valve for a partly closed gate, and look at the pipe itself. Galvanized pipe can lose half its opening to rust scale. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Orlando, FL

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Orlando

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. Shallow spot repairs cost the least. Most single-leak repairs land between $300 and $800, while a full main line replacement can run $1,500 to $6,000 or more depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    Small spot repairs often do not need one. Full replacements usually do. Rules vary by jurisdiction in Orlando, so HDF Plumbing checks the local requirement for your address and pulls the permit when one applies. An inspection happens with the pipe still exposed.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Orlando, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, which most residential service lines are.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, including the locate and backfill. Full replacements are a day. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. Replacements usually take a day. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, though digging the two pits and setting up the boring equipment takes a few hours on its own before any pipe moves. In most cases your water is back on before evening.
