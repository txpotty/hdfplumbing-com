    What We Install in Orlando, FL

    Expansion Tank and Sewage Ejector Installs in Orlando, FL

    A closed water system has nowhere for heated water to expand, so pressure climbs every single time the heater cycles. The expansion tank takes up that swing so the relief valve stops weeping. Homes with a PRV usually need one. For basement baths and guest suites below the sewer, we also install ejector systems with a sealed basin, check valve and vent.

    Softener Loop and Conditioner Installs in Orlando

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving stubborn scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. Some homes already have a softener loop plumbed in, usually in the garage, which turns the install into a two-hour job. Without a loop, we tie into the main and build one. Figure on a few extra hours for that. A salt-based softener or a salt-free conditioner fits the same loop.

    Natural Gas and Propane Lines in Orlando, FL

    Natural gas and propane run at different pressures, and the pipe sizing changes with them. We size every run from the appliance BTU ratings and total pipe length, and use black iron or CSST depending on the path. Code requires CSST to be bonded. Skip the bond and a nearby lightning strike can arc through the tubing, which is one more reason gas work is not a weekend project.

    Sump Pump Installation in Orlando, FL

    A sump pump that fails during a storm usually failed months earlier: a stuck float, a seized impeller, a dead check valve or a cord unplugged for the shop vac. We dig the pit to the right depth, set a primary pump sized to the inflow and add a battery backup if you want one. Where the water exits matters most. We run the discharge line far enough from the foundation that the water does not cycle straight back into the pit.

    Water Heater Replacement in Orlando

    Most tank heaters give out between 8 and 12 years, sooner with heavy sediment. Our stock covers 40 and 50 gallon tanks and 75 gallon units for larger families, and each install includes new connectors, a code relief line and a vent check. An expansion tank check is part of every job. One visit covers most installs.

    Water Line Installation and Replacement in Orlando

    Corroded galvanized and brittle polybutylene are the two pipes we replace most. New lines go in as PEX or Type L copper, with crimp or expansion fittings on the plastic and solder on the copper. Nearly all homes in Florida sit on concrete slabs over sandy soil, so leaks under the slab are a common and costly repair. Buried pipe gets sand bedding and proper depth, so rocks and settling do not cut into it later. Above 80 psi, we add a pressure reducing valve.

    PEX Repiping in Orlando

    Copper repipes cost more and take longer, which is why most whole-house repipes today use PEX-A or PEX-B tubing with brass or poly fittings. It runs quieter than copper, with less water hammer banging in the walls. Freeze damage is less likely too. When a rare freeze is forecast, covering outdoor valves and letting a faucet drip typically does the job to prevent damage. We use fittings matched to the tubing brand.

    Tank and Tankless Water Heater Installs in Orlando

    Bradford White and Rheem both make good tanks, and the brand matters less than the fit. We look at hot water demand, the space you have and whether gas or electric is cheaper to run at your house. Then we check the Florida code items. Closed systems, the kind with a check valve or PRV at the meter, usually need an expansion tank, which we install alongside the heater.
