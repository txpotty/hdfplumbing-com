    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Orlando

    The cost of sewage ejector pump installation in Orlando, FL typically ranges from $2,500 to $6,000, depending on pump type, basin size, excavation needs and whether new electrical circuits are required. This estimate includes the pump unit, labor and materials. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after an on-site assessment.

    A sewage ejector pump is designed to handle solid waste and wastewater from plumbing fixtures below the main sewer line, like toilets and sinks, discharging it into the sewer system. A sump pump, in contrast, removes groundwater from a basement or crawl space, only handling clear water to prevent flooding. They serve different purposes for different types of water.

    Regular maintenance for a sewage ejector pump involves periodically checking the basin for debris, confirming the check valve works correctly and testing the float switch. It is also wise to avoid flushing non-biodegradable items. HDF Plumbing offers maintenance services to keep your pump running efficiently and prevent surprise breakdowns.

    Installing a sewage ejector pump is a complex job that involves plumbing, electrical and sometimes excavation work. It requires specialized knowledge to get sizing, venting and code compliance right. We strongly recommend hiring a licensed professional like HDF Plumbing to avoid costly errors, safety hazards and potential code violations.

    Pump replacements typically take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can catch potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is particularly important for older units.

    Most ejector pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. Flushing wipes or other non-flushable items can shorten that life significantly.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional check your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.
