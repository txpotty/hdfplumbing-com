    Frequently Asked Questions About Bathtub Faucet Replacement in Margate

    Most tub faucet replacements run $250 to $600 with a standard faucet. A trim and cartridge swap on a valve that stays in the wall sits near the low end, while replacing the valve itself, especially behind tile with no access panel, lands near the top. The price is set before work starts.

    Replace cartridges before they fail badly. Fix a small drip or a stiffening handle early, before the seat or valve body is damaged. Small fixes stay small.

    A slip-on spout is a fair DIY job. Anything inside the wall is different, because replacing a valve means soldering or crimping in a tight cavity full of wood and then pressure-testing it before the wall closes, and a mistake there leaks out of sight. Leave valves to a plumber.

    Repair swaps the parts inside the valve. A cartridge, stem, seat or washer gets replaced and the valve body stays in the wall, while replacement takes out the valve body itself and puts in a new one, usually with anti-scald protection. Repair costs much less.

    Not always. If there is an access panel or a closet behind the tub, we work from the back, and from the tile side we cut the smallest opening we can and cover it with an oversize trim plate or a remodel plate. Retiling is rare.

    Wall-mount valves with a spout are most common. Deck fillers mount on the rim, floor fillers stand beside the tub, and combo valves use a diverter. Each has its own rough-in.

    The diverter is wearing out. On a spout diverter the rubber gate inside no longer seals, and on a three-handle faucet the middle stem washer has worn, so water splits between the spout and the shower head instead of going all to one. A new diverter spout costs little.

    Do not crank the handle shut. Cartridge valves seal with light pressure, and forcing the handle past the stop wears the seals and the stop itself, so a gentle turn and an occasional wipe of the trim with a soft cloth go a long way. Abrasive cleaners damage finishes.
