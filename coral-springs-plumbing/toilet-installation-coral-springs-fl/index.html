    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Coral Springs

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Coral Springs typically falls between $250 and $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. A rotted flange or a soft subfloor can push the total higher, and we price that work before it starts instead of surprising you on the invoice.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most homes measure 12 inches. Some older homes and tight bathrooms have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet built for the wrong distance either will not fit against the wall or leaves an awkward gap behind the tank. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Plenty of handy homeowners do. The common mistakes are overtightened bolts and a bowl that was never leveled. A cracked foot from overtightening can show up days later, and a rocking bowl lets water soak the subfloor out of sight. On new floors or wall-hung units, hire it out.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Then pick round or elongated. An elongated bowl is roomier but adds about two inches of length, and that matters in a tight half bath. Check the MaP flush score too.

    Lower water bills come first. Pre-1994 toilets can use nearly three times what a 1.28 gallon model does. Newer bowls also have wider glazed trapways that clog less, and the smoother porcelain stays cleaner between scrubbings than an old pitted bowl ever did.

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving heavy scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. Hard water leaves scale in the rim jets and on the fill valve over time. Weak flushes follow. A toilet with a well-glazed trapway and an easy-to-reach fill valve holds up better, and a cup of vinegar left in the overflow tube overnight now and then keeps the rim jets open.

    Watch the base and the tank. A puddle that returns after wiping means a crack or a bad seal. A seal can be replaced for far less than a new toilet, so we check that first, but a crack in the china means replacement, since there is no lasting repair for cracked porcelain.
