    What Goes Into a PEX Job in Coral Springs, FL

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Coral Springs

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. After 40 or 50 years the bore is so choked with scale that a half-inch line delivers a trickle, and the water comes out tinted brown after it sits overnight. We replace every foot of it with PEX. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Coral Springs

    Low pressure at every faucet, not just one, usually points at the pipe and not the fixtures. In older homes it is rust-choked galvanized or polybutylene with failing fittings, and swapping a faucet cartridge will not help. Repiping does fix it. We replace every supply line from the main valve to each angle stop and test the system before closing anything.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Coral Springs

    The main from the meter to the house is the one line everything else depends on. When an old galvanized or polybutylene service starts leaking underground, the yard stays soggy, the water bill climbs, and the pressure drops at every tap. New PEX goes in its own trench. At the house we add a new full-port ball valve for the main shutoff.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Coral Springs, FL

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A bends the easiest and uses expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and uses crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C handles more like B. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    Remodel and Addition PEX Rough-In in Coral Springs

    Rough-in heights matter more than people think. If the shower valve sits two inches low or the vanity stubs miss the cabinet, the wall gets reopened after tile, and that costs time and money. We confirm heights with your contractor first. Then we pressure test with the walls open.

    Leak Repair with PEX in Coral Springs, FL

    A single bad section does not always mean a repipe. We can cut out a split or corroded stretch of copper, transition to PEX with a listed fitting, and have the water back on in an hour or two.     PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Coral Springs

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.
