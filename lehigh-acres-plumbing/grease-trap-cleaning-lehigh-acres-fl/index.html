    Watch the floor sinks during dish rush. Water that lingers in them is an early warning. Other signs include a rancid smell near the dish station, grease showing up in the cleanout nearest the street, and drains that gurgle after the dish machine empties.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Monthly pumping is common for busy restaurants. Lighter kitchens, like a coffee shop with a small food menu, can stretch to every two or three months as long as depth readings stay under the 25 percent line.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Generally a trap is the small unit indoors, often under 100 gallons. An interceptor is the big in-ground tank outside, often 500 gallons or more, and plumbing codes treat them as separate categories with different sizing rules.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Longer term, a hardened grease cap that can take hours to break up. The hydrogen sulfide from rotting grease eats concrete too, and a neglected interceptor can need new baffles and risers years early.
