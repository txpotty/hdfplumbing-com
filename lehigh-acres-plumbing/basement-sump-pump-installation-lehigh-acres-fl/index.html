    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Lehigh Acres

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    We install a wide range of high-quality, reliable sump pump brands, including Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps. We can help you pick the best brand and model for your budget and water management needs in your Lehigh Acres home, considering horsepower and features.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained once a year, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and confirming the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and lengthens the life of your pump.

    HDF Plumbing provides a warranty on our labor for basement sump pump installations, typically for one year. The sump pump itself comes with a manufacturer's warranty, which can range from 1 to 5 years depending on the brand and model. We will provide you all warranty details at the time of installation.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also red flags.

    Many homes in Florida built before the mid 1970s have cast iron drains under the slab, which often crack and corrode with age. Homes built on slabs without basements seldom need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water quickly during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    We routinely install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will fit the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain. If your pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, it is also wise to consider a replacement before it fails completely.
