    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Fountainebleau

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. That alone takes 30 to 40 minutes. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Any weeping at the dielectric nipples goes on the report.

    Expansion Tank Service in Fountainebleau, FL

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. Our tech checks the air charge with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Fountainebleau, FL

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.

    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Fountainebleau, FL

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Electric Element Check in Fountainebleau

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. We test each one with a meter. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Fountainebleau

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. Never plug or cap that pipe.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Fountainebleau, FL

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Three to five years is typical. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. Swapping a $40 to $80 rod is a lot cheaper than a new tank, and on a tank under eight years old it is usually the best money you can spend on it.

    Relief Valve Testing in Fountainebleau

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. If it keeps dripping, we replace it. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.
