    Plumbing leaks show up no matter the weather. Seepage comes and goes with rain. A moving meter with everything off means a pipe leak, and water that shows up after storms means groundwater.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Keep them clear and extended well away from the foundation. Test the sump each spring and swap rubber washer hoses for braided ones every five years.

    Look for damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, and white chalky deposits on concrete. Those deposits are efflorescence, left behind when water evaporates. A rising water bill or rust at the water heater base points toward a plumbing leak. Smell often comes first.
