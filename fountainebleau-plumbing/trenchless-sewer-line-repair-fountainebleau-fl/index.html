    Sewer Camera Inspection for Trenchless Repair in Fountainebleau

    Every trenchless job starts with a push camera fed in from the cleanout or a pulled toilet.     Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Fountainebleau, FL

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints separate, crack or drop out of grade over the years. Nearly all homes in Florida sit on concrete slabs over sandy soil, so leaks under the slab are a common and costly repair. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Then age finishes the job. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under load.

    Pipe Bursting in Fountainebleau, FL

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Cost Comparison of Trenchless vs Traditional in Fountainebleau, FL

    Depth changes the math. A shallow line in open lawn is cheap to dig, but a lateral eight feet deep that runs under the driveway and a porch costs far more to excavate than to burst or line from two pits. You get both prices on paper. Then you pick.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Prep and Lining in Fountainebleau

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. Skip that step and the liner can wrinkle or fail to bond. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair Costs in Fountainebleau, FL

    Compare total cost, not just the plumbing line on the invoice. Sod and sprinkler repair add up too.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Replacement in Fountainebleau

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. Over time it softens, goes oval and blisters on the inside, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Clay that still holds its round shape is a good candidate for a liner.
