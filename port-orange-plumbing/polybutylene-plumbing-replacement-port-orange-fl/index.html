    How We Replace Polybutylene in Port Orange, FL

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Port Orange

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. For slab houses, new PEX-A runs through the attic and drops down interior walls, so none of it sits under the floor. It also tolerates freezing better. Expansion fittings keep the bore full size, which helps pressure at the last fixture on the line.

    Full Home Polybutylene Repiping in Port Orange

    A full replacement takes every poly supply line out of service, not just the sections that have failed. Leaving some poly keeps the risk. We run new lines from the main shutoff to every sink, toilet, tub, washer, ice maker and water heater, add new quarter-turn stops and test the whole system before patching.

    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Port Orange, FL

    Some homeowners in Port Orange, FL want copper when they take out polybutylene. That works fine. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, and join it with lead-free solder or press fittings. Expect a higher price and a slightly longer job with copper, in exchange for good heat and UV tolerance and a long history.

    The Cost of Polybutylene Replacement in Port Orange, FL

    Price depends mostly on house size, the number of bathrooms, attic access and whether you pick PEX or copper. PEX costs less. A planned replacement usually costs less than the drywall, flooring and cabinet repairs after a single burst, and that is before any deductible or mold cleanup gets added on. Our estimate lists labor, materials, permit and patching separately.

    Whole-Home PEX Replacement in Port Orange, FL

    PEX-A is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, scale resistant and easy to route. We use Uponor PEX-A with expansion fittings that do not restrict flow. New lines run from the main shutoff to every fixture, water heater and appliance, and most homes are completed in two to four working days with water back on each night.
