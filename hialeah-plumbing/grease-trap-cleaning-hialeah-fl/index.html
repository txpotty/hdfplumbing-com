    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Hialeah, FL

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. The service covers grease and solids removal, a baffle scrape, a look at the inlet flow control fitting, and a new lid gasket if the old one is flattened. A bad gasket is why most of these smell.

    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Hialeah, FL

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. Every compartment gets opened and pumped to the bottom. With the tank empty, we inspect the tees, baffle wall, risers and lid seals, since a cracked riser lets dirt and groundwater into the tank. Lids go back on tight.

    Preventive Maintenance Programs in Hialeah, FL

    A maintenance plan is just a pumping interval plus someone who tracks it. We keep the calendar so your manager does not have to. When the depth reads near 20 percent at a visit, the next date moves up, and when it reads 10 percent, it moves back so you skip an unneeded pump-out. Adjustments happen at every visit.

    Odor Control and Trap Inspections in Hialeah

    A trap that stinks between pump-outs usually has a flattened lid gasket or a vent problem. Both get checked every visit. Gaskets are cheap to swap, but a broken vent can push sewer gas into the kitchen and slow the drain too, so it is worth chasing. Most smells get fixed that visit.

    Residential Grease Trap Cleaning in Hialeah, FL

    Homes rarely have a grease trap, though a few large custom kitchens and some home-based catering setups in Hialeah, FL put a small unit under the sink. Most are 10 to 20 gallon boxes.     Grease Trap Pumping and Hauling in Hialeah

    Trap waste is regulated as a liquid waste, so in most places it has to be hauled by a licensed hauler to an approved receiving facility, not dumped down a manhole or into a dumpster. Pumping and hauling are both on us. Everything goes into a sealed tank on the truck, and each load is logged with the gallons and where it went. No barrels left behind.
