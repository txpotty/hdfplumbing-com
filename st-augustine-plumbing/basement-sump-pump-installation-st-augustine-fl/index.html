    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in St. Augustine

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in St. Augustine. You get a written price after we look.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. New pits and backup systems are a different matter, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Those belong to a pro.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. Our labor has its own warranty on top of that, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. After about ten years, plan on replacing it.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Hurricanes and tropical storms bring flooding, power loss, and sewer backups to Florida, often overwhelming drainage systems for days.
