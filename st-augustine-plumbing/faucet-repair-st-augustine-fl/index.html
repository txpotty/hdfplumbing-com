    Faucet Handle Repair and Replacement in St. Augustine, FL

    Stripped handle splines, cracked handle bodies, worn adapters and loose set screws make a faucet hard to turn or impossible to shut tight. Many brands sell the handle on its own.     Faucet Repair Versus Replacement in St. Augustine, FL

    A new cartridge usually costs well under the price of a new faucet and can add years of life, as long as the faucet body is sound and parts are still made. Cracked or discontinued faucets are different. You get a repair price and a replacement price so the choice is yours.

    Compression Faucet Repair in St. Augustine

    Two-handle faucets with separate hot and cold handles are often compression faucets, which seal by pressing a rubber washer down onto a brass seat inside the valve. Washers harden and seats get pitted. New washers go in and the seat gets dressed or replaced with a seat wrench, and the packing gets redone if the stem leaks.

    Pull-Down Kitchen Faucet Repair in St. Augustine

    Pull-down kitchen faucets wear at the hose, the spray head and the docking magnet or counterweight that pulls the hose back into the spout. Hoses split near the quick-connect. We replace hoses, spray heads and weights with brand parts, then check for drips under the sink where the hose connects.

    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in St. Augustine

    Sputtering flow, where the water spits and surges, often means air or debris in the line after a water main repair, or a partly clogged cartridge screen. Flushing usually clears it. On pull-down faucets, the check valves in the hose can stick too, and we clean or replace those.

    Ball Faucet Repair in St. Augustine, FL

    Ball faucets have a single lever over a domed cap, and the drip usually comes from the two small rubber seats and springs that press against the ball. A seats and springs kit fixes most of them. Loose handles happen too. Tightening the adjusting ring under the cap usually stops a handle leak.

    Dripping Faucet Repair in St. Augustine

    One drip a second adds up to more than 3,000 gallons a year, roughly the water used in 180 showers. That waste lands on your water bill. We find the worn washer, O-ring or cartridge and replace it, then check the cabinet below for swelling or stains from the slow leak.

    Bathroom Pop-Up Drain Repair in St. Augustine

    Pop-up drains on bathroom sinks fail at the pivot ball, the clevis strap, the lift rod or the stopper itself, and a slow drain often turns out to be hair wrapped around the stopper. Try lifting out the stopper first. We replace the pop-up assembly in a matching finish when the old one is corroded or the pivot leaks.
