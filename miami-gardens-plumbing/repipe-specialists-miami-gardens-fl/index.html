    What Our Repipe Crews Do in Miami Gardens, FL

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Miami Gardens, FL

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. Full replacement is the fix. Many insurers ask about it.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Miami Gardens

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. This works well for a first leak. If the house has had several leaks, we will walk through why a full repipe usually costs less over time.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Miami Gardens

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Miami Gardens

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. We use Type L, not the thinner Type M, and we support it with the right hangers so it does not rub or rattle in the walls. Lead-free solder only. Because some water chemistries eat copper, we look at your leak history before recommending it over PEX.

    Condo, Townhome and Multi-Family Repipes in Miami Gardens

    Shared walls and HOA rules make repiping attached homes more involved. Our office works with your HOA on approvals and shutoff times, and we take steps to protect the units next door. Property managers get phased schedules. Big buildings get repiped by stack or wing, which lets residents stay home.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in Miami Gardens

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it. We stop the active leak first, make temporary repairs that get water back on and then plan the full repipe so you are not rushed into it. That gives you time. Your policy may pay for water damage even if not for the new pipe, so we take photos of everything we find.

    Drain Line Repiping in Miami Gardens

    Drain pipe wears out too, especially old cast iron that cracks and scales up inside. Slow drains in several fixtures, sewer odors and repeated clogs in the same line are common signs of a failing drain system. A camera shows the condition. Failing sections are replaced with PVC or ABS sloped and vented to code, which drains faster and resists buildup much better than the pipe it replaces.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Miami Gardens, FL

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Both prices include permits, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Miami Gardens

    Most whole-home repipes in Miami Gardens, FL fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. Square footage, fixture count, material choice and the amount of drywall work along the route set the final price. We quote it in writing.

    Our repipes come with a written workmanship warranty on parts and labor, usually covering several years. Manufacturer coverage adds more. Certified PEX-A installs carry the manufacturer's long-term warranty on the pipe and fittings, and the exact terms of both warranties are on your estimate.

    Most homes are repiped in two to four days, plus a day for drywall patching. You will have water most nights.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. For PEX, we use Uponor PEX-A most often. We also install Viega and Zurn PEX on some jobs, but we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Having the repipe documented with a permit can make your home easier to sell and insure, especially when replacing polybutylene or Kitec. Inspectors look for it.

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving stubborn scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Pressure falls as scale builds. In metal pipe, scale also speeds up corrosion, so repiping with PEX-A and adding a softener or filter during the job can protect the new plumbing and your fixtures for years.

    Rusty or brown water, low pressure at several fixtures and repeated pinhole leaks are the classic signs. Banging pipes are a clue too. If your home has galvanized steel, polybutylene or Kitec, a repipe is often worth planning before a failure forces the issue, since those materials tend to fail across the whole house rather than in one spot.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper is durable but costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.
