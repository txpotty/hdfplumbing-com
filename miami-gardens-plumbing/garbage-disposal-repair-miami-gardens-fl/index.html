    What Goes Wrong on a Disposal, and How We Fix It in Miami Gardens, FL

    Grinding Noise Repair in Miami Gardens

    Loud metal-on-metal sounds may mean a loose mount, a foreign object or worn impellers. We inspect the chamber, tighten the mounting ring and remove any debris, then run the unit empty to hear whether the motor itself is making the noise. Bad bearings mean replacement.

    Disposal Reset and Electrical Repair in Miami Gardens

    When a disposal will not turn on at all, the thermal overload may have tripped or the switch may be bad. We check the reset button, breaker, wall switch and wiring, and we also test air switches and batch-feed lids that use a magnet to start the motor. Most are simple fixes.

    Slow Draining Disposal Repair in Miami Gardens, FL

    Fine grinding with slow draining usually means the discharge tube or the trap arm is packed. Rice and coffee grounds are the usual culprits. We pull the trap, clear the tube and the arm with the right size cable, and check that the arm has at least a quarter inch of fall per foot toward the wall.

    Leak Detection and Repair in Miami Gardens, FL

    There are four usual leak points on a disposal: the sink flange, the dishwasher inlet, the discharge tube and the housing itself. We dry everything, run water with a little food coloring and watch to see which joint shows color first, since water runs down the outside of the unit and hides the real source. Three of the four are fixable. A leak from the housing means it is time for a new unit.

    Emergency Disposal and Sink Backup Repair in Miami Gardens

    When a disposal fails, water can back into the sink and the dishwasher. If water is rising or reaching the floor, shut off the disposal and the dishwasher and call us, since running either one just adds more water to the cabinet. We move quickly on these.

    Jam Clearing and Motor Repair in Miami Gardens

    Bones, fruit pits and bottle caps wedge between the impeller lugs and the grind ring, and the motor stalls. We shut off power, insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely. Next we pull the object with pliers. Long stalls can overheat the motor, so we run it under load before we finish.

    Commercial Disposal Repair in Miami Gardens

    Restaurant disposals run 1 to 5 horsepower and usually include a control box and a solenoid valve for water. When one stops, the dish line backs up fast. We check the controls first. A failed solenoid or a tripped overload in the control panel stops the unit just as completely as a jam, and it is a much faster fix.
