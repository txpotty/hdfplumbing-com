    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, generally more than about ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.

    It removes the water mold needs, so new growth slows down. It does not kill mold that is already there. Any mold already growing needs to be remediated before or alongside the waterproofing, and once the space is dry and sealed it should stay that way.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to the method we use, the quality of the install and the soil conditions around your house. Sump pumps wear out sooner. Plan on a new pump every seven to ten years.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, heavy equipment, correct product thickness and an understanding of how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.

    Bubbling paint is a big one. Soft drywall near the floor, stained baseboards, a musty smell and a chalky white crust on block or stucco all point to moisture, and in a shower, loose tiles or cracked grout usually mean water is already behind the wall. A moisture meter confirms it in minutes.
