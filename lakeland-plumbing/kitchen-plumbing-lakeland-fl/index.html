    New Disposal Installation in Lakeland

    Dishwasher and Ice Maker Lines in Lakeland

    A dishwasher needs a hot supply with its own shutoff, a drain to the disposal or a wye tailpiece, and either an air gap at the sink or a high loop under the counter, depending on local code. Fridges need a cold line. We run braided stainless or copper to the fridge with a quarter-turn shutoff, never the thin plastic tubing that splits behind the fridge.

    Grease Trap Installation in Lakeland, FL

    Small kitchens can usually get by with an under-sink trap, while full restaurants often need an in-ground interceptor sized to the local sewer authority's rules. The local rule gets checked before we price it. Sizing is based on fixture flow, and an undersized trap fills fast and lets grease pass straight into the sewer line.

    Burst Supply Lines in Lakeland, FL

    Plastic supply lines with plastic nuts crack more often than braided stainless, especially after years of pressure swings. A failed line under the sink can put gallons per minute into the cabinet and the floor below before anyone notices it. We replace both sides. The second line is just as old as the one that failed.

    Disposal Replacement in Lakeland, FL

    Disposals last about eight to twelve years. When the housing starts weeping from the bottom, it is done. We drop the old unit, check the mounting ring, install the new one and reconnect the dishwasher hose using a fresh clamp so it does not leak at the inlet. We haul the old one away.

    Hard Water and the Kitchen in Lakeland, FL

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving stubborn scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. The aerator is where you see scale first. A faucet that loses pressure over a few months usually has a clogged aerator screen or a scaled cartridge, and we clean or replace both. We can also test your water hardness and price a softener or an under-sink filter.

    Replacing an Old Kitchen Sink in Lakeland

    Pulling an old cast iron sink takes two people and a putty knife, since they are heavy and usually caulked in tight. We check the cutout first. A new sink has to match that opening or the counter has to be trimmed, and we tell you which before you buy. The drain gets rebuilt to suit the new bowl depth.

    Disposal Jams and Hums in Lakeland, FL

    A hum without grinding tells us the motor has power and the plate will not turn. That is often ten minutes. We free the plate from below with a hex key, clear the hopper with pliers, never a hand, and reset the overload before running water through it. No sound at all points us to the switch, the outlet or the breaker.
