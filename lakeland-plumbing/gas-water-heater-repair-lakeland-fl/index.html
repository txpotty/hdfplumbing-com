    What Our Gas Water Heater Repairs Cover in Lakeland, FL

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Lakeland

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. A flame arrestor screen at the base lets air in and keeps flame from getting out. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. The screen and the chamber both get cleaned. We replace any tripped thermal switch.

    Pilot Light Repair in Lakeland

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Figure on roughly an hour for most pilot repairs.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Lakeland

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Lakeland home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Lakeland, FL

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. The tech reads the code and tests the thermopile and sensors before condemning the valve. Sometimes the valve really is bad. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. We leave the old valve with you if you ask.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Lakeland

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Then each part gets a meter test.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Lakeland, FL

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. Plan on roughly an hour for the swap. Every joint on the new valve gets leak tested.
