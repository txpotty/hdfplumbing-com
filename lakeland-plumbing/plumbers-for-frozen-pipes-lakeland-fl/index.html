    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Lakeland

    Costs range from about $200 for simple thawing to $1,500 or more for a burst pipe inside a finished wall. Access drives the price. Open crawlspace lines are quick, and ceiling repairs take more time and drywall work.

    Shut off the main water valve right away. Turn off power or gas to the water heater next. Move belongings, drain the lines from a low faucet, and call us for the repair.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Seal and insulate around exposed lines. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. A toilet that will not refill or a bulging pipe also points to ice.

    No pipe is freeze-proof. PEX tolerates freezing better than most. It can stretch slightly as ice forms instead of splitting the way copper does, but the fittings at each end can still crack, so location and insulation matter more than the pipe itself.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Pipes inside walls take more time. A frozen line inside an exterior wall or under a floor can take a few hours, especially if it is PEX or CPVC, which we thaw with gentler infrared or warm air instead of an electric thawing machine.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. Water drains out of the stem after shutoff. Leaving a hose attached defeats it, since trapped water can split the valve inside the wall.
