    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in St. Petersburg

    A simple indoor gas line for a range or dryer typically costs $350 to $1,000. Outdoor runs to a grill, fire pit or pool heater often range from $1,000 to $3,500 depending on distance, trenching and line size.

    HDF Plumbing stands behind our work with a comprehensive warranty on our gas line installations. This usually covers workmanship for a set period and may include manufacturer warranties on materials. Specific warranty details will be provided with your project estimate.

    We mainly install black iron pipe and corrugated stainless steel tubing, or CSST. Black iron is a traditional, rigid option, while CSST offers flexibility and faster installation. Both are safe and code-compliant when installed by licensed professionals, and the choice usually depends on the application and property layout.

    Gas lines usually do not need frequent inspections once installed correctly, but it is good practice to have them checked during routine plumbing inspections or if you notice unusual smells or sounds. Newer homes across Florida mostly use PEX or CPVC with PVC drains, though copper pinhole leaks are still a frequent repair in older homes. For older homes in St. Petersburg, an inspection every 5 to 10 years can help catch potential issues.

    Gas line installation is extremely dangerous and should only be performed by licensed professionals. Improper installation can lead to gas leaks, explosions, fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. It also voids warranties and often fails local inspections, so it is never a DIY project.

    The most critical sign of a gas line issue is the smell of rotten eggs, which indicates a gas leak. Other signs include a hissing sound near a gas line, visible damage to a pipe or dead vegetation over an underground gas line. If you suspect a gas leak, evacuate immediately and call your gas provider and then us.

    Most residential gas line installations for a single appliance can be completed in a few hours to one day. More complex projects, such as extending lines for outdoor kitchens or multiple appliances, might take one to two days. This timeline does not include permitting and inspections, which can add several days to the schedule.

    A rotten egg or sulfur smell, a hissing sound near a line or appliance, and dead vegetation over a buried line are red flags. If you smell gas, leave the home and call your gas utility immediately.
