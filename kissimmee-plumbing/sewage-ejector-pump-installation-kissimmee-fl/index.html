    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Kissimmee

    The cost for sewage ejector pump installation in Kissimmee typically ranges from $2,500 to $5,000. This price can vary based on the pump type, basin size, complexity of the plumbing and electrical work and whether an existing system needs to be removed. We provide a detailed quote after assessing your project.

    Regular maintenance for a sewage ejector pump involves periodically checking the basin for debris, confirming the check valve works correctly and testing the float switch. It is also wise to avoid flushing non-biodegradable items. HDF Plumbing offers maintenance services to keep your pump running efficiently and prevent surprise breakdowns.

    Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products, grease and cat litter. These items clog pumps and cause premature failure.

    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is specifically engineered to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve separate purposes.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can catch potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is especially important for older units.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can affect its lifespan. Annual inspections can help maximize its longevity.

    New ejector installations usually require a plumbing permit and inspection. Straight pump replacements may not, and we confirm local requirements for each job.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional inspect your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.
