    Pinhole Leak Repair in North Miami

    Pinholes start inside the pipe where the copper wall pits and thins. The first sign is often a spot. They are small and easy to miss behind drywall, so we locate them with a thermal camera and moisture meter before cutting a single opening in the wall or ceiling. We replace the pitted length with Type L. Nearby pipe gets checked.

    Hot Water Copper Line Repair in North Miami, FL

    Hot water lines corrode faster than cold lines. Heat speeds it up. Recirculation pumps that run around the clock push water through the copper at high velocity and wear it from the inside, which is why we often add a timer to the pump after fixing the leak. It slows the next pinhole.

    Copper Fitting and Valve Repair in North Miami

    Angle stops, shutoff valves and copper fittings under sinks and behind toilets can weep or seize. Scale locks them up. Hard water buildup inside a valve can keep it from closing when you need it most, so we replace failing valves and fittings with quarter-turn ball valves that shut off fully and are easy to turn. Every valve gets checked.

    Copper Slab Leak Repair in North Miami

    Many slab homes have copper water lines running under the concrete. Sometimes you hear it. When those lines leak, you may notice warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water or a meter that turns with every fixture off, so we find it electronically first and then quote a slab repair or a reroute. We price each option.

    Thin-Wall Copper Pinhole Repair in North Miami

    Older copper, especially Type M, pinholes more than heavier pipe. There is less metal to lose. We locate the leak precisely and either solder in a new section or use a press fitting near framing, with new pipe that is heavier than the original. We check the rest of the run.

    Preventive Copper Pipe Inspection in North Miami, FL

    An inspection catches thin spots before they leak. It takes about an hour. We look at exposed copper for green crust and pitting, test static pressure, check the bond wire at the water heater and main, and run a thermal scan on walls where pipe runs if the house has had leaks before.
