    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Installation in Palm Bay

    Most residential water line installations run $1,800 to $5,500 depending on length, soil and material. Long runs through rocky ground, trenchless boring or copper pipe can push costs to $6,000 to $10,000 or more.

    Some homeowners dig their own trench, but connecting to the meter, sizing the pipe and passing inspection are best handled by a licensed plumber. A poor joint underground can leak for months before anyone notices.

    Common signs include persistently low water pressure throughout your home, discolored or rusty water from your taps, frequent leaks in your yard or near the foundation, or unusually high water bills without a clear cause. These usually indicate an aging or failing main water line.

    For most homes, PEX and copper are generally the best materials. PEX is flexible, corrosion-resistant and handles minor ground shifts well. Copper is very durable, long-lasting and resists bacterial growth. Many wells in Florida produce water with a sulfur smell or high iron, which often calls for filtration to protect fixtures and appliances. The choice usually depends on budget, water chemistry and site conditions.

    Depth requirements vary by jurisdiction and climate. When a rare freeze is forecast, covering outdoor valves and letting a faucet drip typically does the job to prevent damage. Lines must be buried below the local frost line, so colder areas call for deeper trenches than mild ones.

    Type K copper, PEX and HDPE are all accepted choices. HDPE and PEX flex with soil movement and have fewer joints, while copper offers rigidity and puncture resistance.
