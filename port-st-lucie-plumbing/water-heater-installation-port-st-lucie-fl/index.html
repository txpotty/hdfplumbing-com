    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Port St. Lucie

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Plumbing work in Florida follows the Florida Building Code, whose plumbing volume is based on the International Plumbing Code. A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs that extra volume, which keeps the relief valve from weeping and saves wear on faucets and fittings. We pre-charge it to your house pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Port St. Lucie, FL

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. When the water heats and expands with nowhere to go, pressure climbs and stresses the relief valve, the toilet fill valves and every supply line in the house. An expansion tank absorbs it. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Electric Water Heater Installation in Port St. Lucie, FL

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. We confirm the breaker and wire size match the new unit before connecting it, since many older homes have the wrong gauge. No venting needed. You get more spots to put it.

    Tankless Water Heater Installation in Port St. Lucie, FL

    Going tankless means the heater fires only when a tap opens, and it never runs out. Gas tankless models usually need a bigger gas line than the old tank had, plus a dedicated vent run through the wall or roof. Upfront cost is higher. We confirm your meter and line can handle it first.

    Electric Tank Water Heater Installation in Port St. Lucie, FL

    Without gas service, an electric tank is the usual pick. We match the voltage and breaker size to your panel, and because electric tanks need no vent, they can go in a closet, an attic or a laundry room where a gas unit could not. A drain pan goes under closet installs. Quiet and simple.

    Hybrid Heat Pump Water Heater Installation in Port St. Lucie, FL

    Heat pump water heaters pull warmth from the surrounding air and can use about a third of the power of a standard electric tank.
