    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in West Palm Beach

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. After-hours calls cost more.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Slow drains, gurgling pipes and sewer smell are the early ones. If the tub fills when the toilet flushes, the main is blocked. That one needs a call. Several fixtures acting up at once almost always points to a main line clog rather than a single trap under one sink.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. We tell you up front. Once the tech sees the cleanout and asks which fixtures are slow, they can give you a closer estimate of how long it will run.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in West Palm Beach's older homes suffer the worst of it. They also put our techs at risk. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Clearing a clog in West Palm Beach does not need a permit. Repair work is different. A new cleanout, a dig to fix broken pipe or a sewer replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. We handle the permit and the inspection.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water rising in a tub or shower when another fixture drains is a strong sign of a main line clog. Act on that quickly. The longer it goes, the more likely the next flush ends with sewage on the bathroom floor.
