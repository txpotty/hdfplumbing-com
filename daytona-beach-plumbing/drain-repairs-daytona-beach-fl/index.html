    Trap and slip-joint repairs typically cost $150 to $350. Pipe sections run higher. A section of drain pipe usually runs $400 to $1,200, and cast iron stack replacement can range from $2,000 to $6,000 with drywall, flooring and access driving most of the spread. We write it up before we start.

    We do. Terms vary by job. A trap replacement and a cast iron stack swap carry different terms because the parts and the risks are different, and your plumber explains exactly what is covered before the work starts. Manufacturer warranties stack on top.

    Cleaning removes a blockage from a sound pipe. Repair changes the pipe itself. A crack, a sag, a broken fitting or a missing vent will keep causing clogs no matter how often the line gets cleaned, which is why recurring clogs usually call for a fix instead of another cleaning. A camera tells you which.

    We advise against them. Most only open a small hole. Harsh cleaners can soften old glue joints, eat at metal drain parts and sit in the trap if they do not clear the clog, leaving caustic liquid in the pipe for the plumber to deal with. Mechanical cleaning is safer.

    Repeat clogs come from something in the pipe catching debris. Usually it is buildup. Poor pipe slope, a missing vent or a cracked fitting with a rough edge catches debris over and over, even after the line is cleared. A camera shows which one you have.

    Snugging a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is a fair DIY job. Do not crank it down. If the leak comes from a cracked tailpiece, a corroded metal drain or somewhere inside the wall, it needs a plumber who can fix the pipe and look for rot nearby. Cabinet rot hides well.
