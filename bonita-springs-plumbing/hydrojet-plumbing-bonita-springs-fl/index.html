    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrojet Plumbing in Bonita Springs

    Residential hydrojetting in Bonita Springs typically costs $400 to $900, including a camera inspection. Commercial lines, long runs or heavy root intrusion can cost $800 to $2,000 or more. We give you a clear price after reviewing the line.

    We use professional cart and trailer jetters from manufacturers like Spartan and RIDGID, paired with rotating, penetrating and root-cutting nozzles. Our cameras record video of the line before and after. This equipment lets us handle both homes and commercial buildings.

    Snaking involves using an auger to punch a hole through a clog, usually leaving behind some debris. Hydrojetting, on the other hand, uses high-pressure water to thoroughly scour the entire inner surface of the pipe, removing buildup and leaving it clean. Hydrojetting provides a much more comprehensive and longer-lasting solution.

    Hydrojetting is very effective against stubborn clogs caused by grease buildup, mineral scale, soap scum, hair, and even tree root intrusions. It can clear nearly any type of blockage that accumulates inside a pipe, restoring full flow.

    We utilize professional-grade hydrojetting equipment from reputable manufacturers like Spartan Tool, US Jetting, and Gorlitz. These brands are known for their reliability, power, and effectiveness in clearing even the toughest pipe blockages, ensuring a thorough clean every time.

    It is safe for most cast iron, clay, ABS and PVC lines in reasonable condition. We always inspect with a camera first and lower the pressure or recommend another method if the pipe is cracked or badly corroded. That step protects weak lines.

    The duration of hydrojet plumbing depends on the complexity of the clog and the length of the pipe being cleaned. Most residential hydrojetting jobs in Bonita Springs can be completed within 1 to 3 hours, but more severe or extensive blockages, especially those involving main sewer lines or heavy root intrusion, can take longer.

    Rental jetters exist, but high pressure can injure you or damage a weak pipe if used incorrectly. Without a camera you also cannot tell whether the line is clean or broken. Professional jetting is safer and generally more effective.
