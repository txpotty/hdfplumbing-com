    Root Intrusion Repair in Bonita Springs, FL

    Cutting roots treats the symptom. The joint is the real problem. After clearing the line, we look at each rooted joint on camera and decide between a short sectional liner, a full liner or cutting in new pipe, based on the number of bad joints and the spacing between them.     A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Sewer gas follows soon after.     Commercial Sewer Repair in Bonita Springs

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Bonita Springs

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. Over decades it goes oval and blisters. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, and once the camera shows failures along the whole run, patching it stops being worth the money. We replace these lines with PVC. Trenchless bursting is often an option.

    Sewer Pipe Relining in Bonita Springs, FL

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line with a jetter first. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, which seals cracks and root entry points in a single continuous tube. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. For most houses the flow difference is negligible.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Bonita Springs

    Most jobs fall between $500 for a small spot repair and about $3,000 for a trenchless section. Replacing the whole line is a bigger number. A complete open trench replacement can reach $10,000 or more, depending on the length of the line, how deep it sits and what has to be removed and restored on top of it. The written price comes after we see the pipe.

    Trenchless repair fixes the pipe through one or two small access pits. The yard stays mostly intact. Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE pipe through the old one while splitting the old pipe apart, and cured-in-place lining inserts a resin-soaked tube that hardens into a new pipe wall inside the old one, which leaves concrete and landscaping alone. We check on camera whether yours does.

    Digging up and replacing a lateral means trenching near gas and electric lines. You will be working in sewage. The new pipe has to hit the right slope, sit on proper bedding and connect with the correct transition fittings, which is why many cities only permit licensed plumbers to do it. Leave the cable machine work to us too.

    We use Schedule 40 PVC or ABS that meets local code. Transitions get shielded couplings. The coupling has to match the outside diameter of both pipes, and for lining work we use epoxy-saturated liners that cure into a rigid, jointless pipe inside the host. All materials are rated for buried sewer use.

    Most spot repairs are done in one day, excavation and backfill included. Bigger jobs run past a day. Larger replacements, or jobs that need a right-of-way permit to dig near the street, usually run two to four days with inspection timing as the main variable. We put the schedule in writing.

    It is a repair done without opening a trench along the whole pipe. Often just one or two holes. Cured-in-place lining and pipe bursting are the two common methods, and both keep most of the yard, the driveway and the trees undisturbed. The video shows whether it will work for you.

    Clearing a soft clog with a rental auger is one thing. Repairing the pipe is another.
