    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Assembly Installation in Bonita Springs

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of backflow protection and are often required for high-hazard applications in Bonita Springs. These devices feature two independent check valves and a relief valve that opens to discharge water if pressure differentials indicate a backflow condition. HDF Plumbing focuses on precise RPZ installation, with proper sizing, placement and testing to meet all regulatory requirements for residential and commercial properties.     Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Bonita Springs

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules allow. We size them to avoid too much pressure loss on your system.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Bonita Springs

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Bonita Springs, FL can range from approximately $300 to $1,500, depending on the type of device required, such as a DCVA or an RPZ, the complexity of the installation and any changes needed to existing plumbing. Commercial installations with larger devices may cost more. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after an on-site assessment.

    Signs of a failing backflow preventer include discolored or foul-smelling water, a noticeable drop in water pressure, visible leaks around the device or water discharging from the relief valve on an RPZ. If you notice any of these, call us for an inspection in Bonita Springs.

    Backflow preventers are installed at cross-connection points where non-potable water could enter the potable water supply. Common locations include irrigation system connections, fire sprinkler lines, boiler systems and connections to auxiliary water supplies, usually just after the main water meter or at the point of hazard.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This confirms the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also help with scheduling these annual tests.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and boilers.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for new backflow assemblies, especially on commercial properties. We check local rules and pull the permit when required.

    It depends on the hazard and local rules. Irrigation often uses a PVB or double check, while high hazard connections like commercial kitchens or chemical injection require an RPZ.
