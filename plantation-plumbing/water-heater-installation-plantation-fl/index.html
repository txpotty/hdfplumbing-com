    Tankless Water Heater Installation in Plantation, FL

    Going tankless means the heater fires only when a tap opens, and it never runs out. Gas tankless models usually need a bigger gas line than the old tank had, plus a dedicated vent run through the wall or roof. Upfront cost is higher. We confirm your meter and line can handle it first.

    Electric Tank Water Heater Installation in Plantation, FL

    Without gas service, an electric tank is the usual pick. We match the voltage and breaker size to your panel, and because electric tanks need no vent, they can go in a closet, an attic or a laundry room where a gas unit could not. A drain pan goes under closet installs. Quiet and simple.

    Gas Tank Water Heater Installation in Plantation

    A gas tank is the most common water heater we install, in 40, 50 and 75 gallon sizes. Each one gets a new gas connector, a sediment trap and a shutoff valve, plus a vent connector pitched up toward the flue at a quarter inch per foot. We test draft before leaving. Gas suits busy households.

    Water Heater Installation Cost in Plantation, FL

    Up front, a tank heater costs less than tankless. Gas tankless installs often run higher because of the bigger gas line and new venting, and attic jobs, relocations or code upgrades add labor on top of that. Permits are included. We compare operating costs so you can choose with real numbers.

    Electric Water Heater Installation in Plantation, FL

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. We confirm the breaker and wire size match the new unit before connecting it, since many older homes have the wrong gauge. No venting needed. That makes placement easier.

    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Plantation

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Insurance companies in Florida often ask about plumbing age and material, so permitted work by a certified plumber can help with coverage. A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs that extra volume, which keeps the relief valve from weeping and saves wear on faucets and fittings. We set the air charge to match your water pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Plantation

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it. Sizing is based on the first-hour rating on the yellow label, which tells you how much hot water the tank can deliver during a busy hour. Gas or electric both work. The fuel hookup, the venting and haul-away are all part of the job.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Plantation

    Water heater installation in Plantation, FL runs about $1,200 to $4,000 or more. The biggest factors are whether it is a tank or tankless unit, its size, its fuel and whether the gas line or panel needs work. Tankless sits at the high end. We quote it in writing after a look.

    A like-for-like tank swap usually takes three to four hours. Converting from tank to tankless, or any job with major gas, vent or electrical changes, can take a full day or more. You will know before we start.

    Tank heaters usually last 10 to 15 years, and tankless units can go 20 or more. Hard water and skipped maintenance shorten that, while yearly flushing and anode checks stretch it a good deal past the average. Flush it once a year.

    Start with correct sizing and a thermostat set at 120 degrees. Insulating the first few feet of hot pipe and flushing the tank once a year both help, and a tankless model cuts standby loss.

    Popping or rumbling, rust-colored water, leaks or an age past ten years are the common clues. Any one of them can mean replacement is near.

    For two to four people, a 40 or 50 gallon tank usually does the job. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by flow rate.

    An expansion tank absorbs the extra pressure created as water heats. Closed systems usually need one by code.
