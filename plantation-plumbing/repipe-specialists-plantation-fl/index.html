    Slab Leak Repiping in Plantation

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Limestone bedrock and sinkhole activity in some areas can shift the ground and crack buried water and sewer lines. Rather than jackhammer to reach one leak, we run new lines through the attic and walls and leave the old under-slab pipe out of service. No jackhammer needed. That stops the current leak and removes the chance of the next one under the same floor.

    Commercial Repipe Services in Plantation

    Older commercial buildings in Plantation often have galvanized risers, copper that has pinholed or plastic pipe that no longer meets code. Our crews swap out risers, branches and drains with as little interruption to tenants as the building allows. Tenants get notice of every shutoff. Work is phased by zone, and we coordinate access, keys and noise limits with the property manager.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Plantation

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. We count fixtures, check attic and crawlspace access, look at where the main comes in and ask about plans for future remodels before we draw a route. Then we size the lines. Bigger homes may need a 1-inch trunk so the shower does not drop when the dishwasher fills.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Plantation

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. Our crews use Uponor or similar PEX-A, where the pipe end gets expanded and shrinks back over the fitting for a full-diameter connection. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Most homes are repiped with PEX-A in two to three days.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Plantation

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. We can usually identify it from the pipe at the water heater, and full replacement makes the house easier to insure and sell.

    Commercial Repiping in Plantation, FL

    Commercial buildings in Plantation, FL often have larger pipe, risers that serve several floors and tenants who cannot lose water for a full day. We phase commercial repipes by floor, suite or riser so most of the building stays in service while we work. Night work is possible. We use Type L copper or commercial-grade PEX sized from fixture units and installed to commercial code.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Plantation, FL

    Around Plantation, flushing the water heater yearly and descaling tankless units helps keep mineral buildup under control. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. For homes with hard water, we usually recommend PEX-A, which resists scale and does not corrode, and we can add a softener loop or a whole-house filter during the repipe. Planning it now saves a second visit later.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Plantation, FL

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Because PEX flexes, it absorbs some of the shock that causes water hammer, and long runs with few fittings mean fewer places for a leak to start inside a wall. Attic PEX gets kept away from flues and out of UV.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Plantation

    PEX repipes in Plantation commonly run $4,000 to $15,000. Copper costs more. Expect copper to land around $8,000 to $25,000 or more, with house size, bathroom count and attic or crawlspace access driving most of the price. You get an itemized quote at the visit.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX costs less, installs faster, handles freezing better and runs quietly, while copper tolerates sunlight and heat better and appeals to owners who want metal pipe, so the right pick depends on your house.

    Around Plantation, flushing the water heater yearly and descaling tankless units helps keep scale buildup under control. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Pressure falls as scale builds. Scale also speeds corrosion in metal lines, which is why PEX-A plus a softener or filter added during a repipe can protect the new system and your fixtures.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. It helps at sale time. Replacing polybutylene, Kitec or old galvanized makes a home easier to insure and easier to sell, and a permit record plus a transferable warranty gives the next owner proof the work was done.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. For PEX, we use Uponor PEX-A most often. Viega and Zurn PEX show up on some jobs, but we keep one brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Most are about the size of a sheet of paper. We cut clean squares at fixtures and along the route, patch every one after the inspection and match the existing texture, so the walls are ready to paint when we finish.

    Fixing one leak at a time works until the leaks start coming every few months. That is the turning point. Each repair means a service call, opened drywall and sometimes an insurance claim, while a repipe replaces all of the aging pipe at once and ends the cycle for decades.

    Most repipes in Plantation, FL take three to five working days for an average house. Big houses take more time. Copper adds time because each turn needs a fitting, and patching usually takes one more day after the plumbing passes.
