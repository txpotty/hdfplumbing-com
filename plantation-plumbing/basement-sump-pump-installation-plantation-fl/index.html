    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Plantation

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Labor on our sump pump installs is warrantied, typically for one year. Each pump also has a factory warranty, usually one to five years depending on the brand and model, and we write the exact coverage on your invoice so you know which part to call about if something fails. Save the paperwork.

    A straight pump replacement generally goes without a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we check the local rules and pull permits when they apply.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. The real answer depends on inflow and lift, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Plantation. A straight pump replacement usually does not, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. We handle the paperwork.

    Liberty Pumps, Zoeller and Wayne are our regulars. All three use cast iron housings and reliable switches, and all three have solid parts support through local supply houses, which matters more than a fancy feature list when something breaks. The pit size picks the model.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. A sticking float or rust streaks on the housing also mean trouble, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Two pumps, two check valves.
