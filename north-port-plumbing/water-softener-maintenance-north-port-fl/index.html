    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Maintenance in North Port

    Standard visits typically cost $125 to $250. A valve rebuild adds roughly $100 to $250. Resin replacement is the big-ticket item at $300 to $700, and it still costs far less than a new softener.

    Check the salt first. A salt level that has not moved in a month usually means the unit has stopped regenerating. Spots on dishes, scale on faucets, stiff laundry and soap that will not lather all point the same direction.

    Evaporated salt pellets are the cleanest option and cause fewer bridges and less sludge than rock salt. Solar salt falls in between. Potassium chloride works if you want to cut sodium, but it softens less per pound, so the salt dose usually has to go up by a quarter or more.

    Scale coming back is the giveaway. You may also notice dull laundry and dry skin. Higher salt use, a brine tank full of water, a grinding noise from the valve head or a drop in water pressure through the house can also mean the softener needs service.

    Topping off salt and cleaning out the brine tank are both reasonable DIY jobs. Resin testing and valve diagnosis are a different story. Those need a titration kit and a valve seal tool, plus some practice reading how a regeneration cycle should look.

    Spots on glassware usually show up first. Towels turn stiff and soap seems to take more to work up a lather. Another tell is a salt level that never goes down, which means the softener is not drawing brine, or a hum from the valve with no cycle change.

    A few inches of water can be normal, depending on the design. A tank that keeps filling higher usually has a clogged injector, a stuck float, a bad refill flow control or a drain line restriction. We clear the cause and reset the valve.
