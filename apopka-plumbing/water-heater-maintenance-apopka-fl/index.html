    Electric Water Heater Element Service in Apopka, FL

    Electric water heaters use upper and lower heating elements controlled by thermostats. Sediment can coat the lower element and cause it to fail. We test both elements and thermostats with the power off and replace parts as necessary. A failed element is often the reason for lukewarm water or running out of hot water quickly. Proper testing avoids replacing parts that are still good.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Maintenance in Apopka

    The cost of water heater maintenance in Apopka, FL typically ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the type of water heater (tank or tankless) and the extent of service required. This usually includes flushing, anode rod inspection, and safety checks. We provide upfront pricing before any work begins.

    The anode rod is a metal rod inside your water heater that sacrifices itself to keep the steel tank from corroding. It attracts corrosive elements in the water. Regular inspection and replacement of a depleted anode rod are crucial for extending the lifespan of your water heater, particularly where water quality varies.

    Popping or rumbling noises, lukewarm water, rusty or smelly hot water, and slow recovery times are typical signs.

    During a water heater flush, we drain the tank completely and then refill it, letting water flow out through the drain valve until it runs clear. This process removes mineral deposits and sediment that collect at the bottom of the tank, which can hurt efficiency and cause damage.

    A typical water heater maintenance service generally takes about 1 to 2 hours, depending on the type of unit and its condition. Tankless water heater descaling might take slightly longer. We work steadily to minimize disruption to your Apopka home.

    Generally, a permit is not required for routine water heater maintenance, since it involves inspections and minor adjustments rather than new installations or major repairs. If a repair during maintenance becomes substantial, we will explain any necessary permits.

    While some basic tasks like flushing the tank might seem DIY-friendly, professional maintenance makes sure all critical components, such as the anode rod, pressure relief valve, and heating elements, are properly inspected. Incorrect DIY methods can damage your unit or void warranties.

    With hard water, flushing once a year is recommended. Homes with very hard water or no softener may benefit from flushing every six months.
