    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Apopka, FL

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. Our plumber load tests the battery instead of just reading its voltage. Next, we cut power to the main pump and watch the backup clear the basin by itself. Alarms get tested too.

    Elevator Pit and Garage Pumps in Apopka

    Elevator pits, parking garages and mechanical rooms all collect water that has nowhere to go by gravity, so a pump has to lift it out. Elevator pit pumps often need oil-detecting controls so hydraulic fluid never gets pumped out with the water. We test duplex systems and high-water alarms on a set schedule. Logs go to the property manager.

    Emergency Sump Pump Diagnostics in Apopka, FL

    Grinding, clicking and a hum with no water moving each point to a different failure. Our plumber works from the outlet and cord to the float and then the motor.     Home Sump Pump Service in Apopka

    A typical house pit is about 18 inches across and two feet deep, and most of what goes wrong happens inside it. We pull the pump, clear the intake screen and scoop out the silt.     Sump Pit Cleaning and Debris Removal in Apopka

    Silt and gravel wash into a pit through the drain tile every time it rains, and over a few years that layer can bury the intake screen. We pump the basin down and scoop out the sediment by hand. A clean pit lets the float move freely. The pump runs cooler too.

    Wayne Sump Pump Maintenance in Apopka, FL

    Some Wayne models draw water through a top suction intake, which helps them avoid pulling in the sludge that settles at the bottom of a pit. That intake gets cleaned, and so does the float mechanism. Loose discharge fittings show up on every brand. Easy fix.

    Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Apopka

    Heavy afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer, and saturated ground can push water into sewer lines and slow every fixture in the house. We test the pump a few weeks before the wet season by filling the pit with a hose until the float trips. Then we time how fast it pumps out and listen to the bearings. That leaves time to replace a weak pump before the first big storm, not during it.
