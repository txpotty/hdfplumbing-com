    Wall Waterproofing Work We Do in Apopka, FL

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Apopka

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, cement board with a membrane or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Apopka

    Newer homes across Florida mostly use PEX or CPVC with PVC drains, though copper pinhole leaks are still a frequent repair in older homes. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Not every basement needs one.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Apopka, FL

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Apopka, FL

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation. Sometimes the fix is a downspout extension and regrading the soil so it slopes away from the house, a drop of about six inches in the first ten feet, and that costs a few hundred dollars. We start with the cheapest fix that will actually work. You get the options in writing, side by side.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Apopka

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. A high water table across much of Florida affects drainage, septic design, and how deeply crews can dig for underground repairs. Backfill goes in last.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Apopka

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Humidity causes condensation on cold lines and clogged AC condensate drains, a frequent source of hidden water damage. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Apopka

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, lapped 12 inches at the seams and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and hold it in place with termination strip. It is a cheap fix that makes a big difference.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Apopka, FL

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Humidity causes condensation on cold lines and clogged AC condensate drains, a frequent source of hidden water damage. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. Most homeowners notice the musty smell is gone within a few weeks. Those weep holes empty into the interior drain if the house has one.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide since soil movement, groundwater levels and how well it was installed all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Drain tile can silt up over time.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to the method we use, the quality of the install and the soil conditions around your house. Sump pumps wear out sooner. Plan on a new pump every seven to ten years.
