    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Town 'n' Country

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. Size, fixtures and attic or crawlspace access set most of the cost, and you get a written quote after we see the house.

    Gray, blue or black plastic pipe marked PB2110 at the water heater or under sinks is the clearest sign. The build year matters. If your home was built between about 1978 and 1995, has this pipe and has had even one leak at a fitting, replacement is strongly recommended before the next one.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail first.

    A full poly repipe in Town 'n' Country, FL usually takes three to seven days. Size is the main factor. At the estimate you get a day-by-day plan, including which rooms come first and when patching happens.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper often goes further. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.

    Whole-home repiping usually requires a permit and inspection. We handle permits and scheduling with your building department.

    Water across much of Florida comes from limestone aquifers and runs hard, leaving heavy scale on fixtures and inside water heaters. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. It adds scale. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.
