    Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair Pricing in Town 'n' Country, FL

    Pipe Bursting in Town 'n' Country, FL

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Sewer Camera Inspection for Trenchless Repair in Town 'n' Country

    Every trenchless job starts with a push camera fed in from the cleanout or a pulled toilet. The screen shows the inside of the pipe live, and a sonde in the camera head lets us mark the exact spot on the ground above it. That tells us the depth. No guessing where to dig.

    Commercial Trenchless Sewer Repair in Town 'n' Country, FL

    Office and retail buildings often have a cast iron building drain under the slab that has rotted along the bottom. Lining it avoids cutting the floor. Work gets scheduled around tenant hours, nights included. A typical commercial lining job runs one to three days, depending on footage and how many branch connections need to be reinstated with a cutter.

    Perma-Liner Lining Equipment in Town 'n' Country

    Perma-Liner makes inversion drums and epoxy systems for lining 2- to 6-inch drain lines, which covers most of what we see in houses. The equipment fits in a pickup bed. Compact gear matters on tight lots. Resin gets mixed on site and timed to the length of the run, so it does not start curing before the liner is in place.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Town 'n' Country, FL

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacements in Town 'n' Country

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. As it moves, it splits the old clay or cast iron and shoves the pieces into the surrounding soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. This is the fix for collapsed pipe.
