    Customer-Supplied Toilet Installation in Ocala, FL

    New Construction Toilet Installation in Ocala

    On new builds we set toilets after the finish floor is down, never before. A flange belongs flush with or just above finished flooring. An early set means the tile goes around the flange, leaving it low, and that calls for an extender ring later. Each bowl gets a fresh seal.

    Commercial Restroom Toilets in Ocala, FL

    Restaurants and offices get heavy-duty fixtures and accessible stalls built to code. A closed restroom costs a business money.     ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Ocala

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. It is set that way so a grab bar fits. We measure the existing drain before ordering, since moving a drain two inches to hit the ADA centerline means opening the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Commercial Flushometer Toilets in Ocala

    Sloan and Zurn flushometer valves are the standard on commercial toilets for a reason. They recover instantly between flushes. Manual and sensor valves go on both floor-mount and wall-hung bowls, with the control stop adjusted so the flush is full but does not splash. Sensor valves cut down on touch points.

    Wall-Hung Carrier Systems in Ocala

    In-wall carriers from Geberit and Grohe each mount a little differently, and the instructions matter. We follow the maker's frame height chart exactly. Set the frame too low and the bowl ends up at child height, too high and nobody's feet touch the floor, and neither can be fixed once the wall is tiled. Measure twice here.

    Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Ocala

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. The tank sits behind an access plate. The carrier holds the whole load, so its feet get anchored to slab or framing and it gets plumbed before any wall finish goes on. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    Comfort-Height and ADA Toilets in Ocala

    Aging in place usually starts in the bathroom. A grab bar and a taller bowl help a lot. If the drywall is already off, blocking gets added between studs so a grab bar can screw into wood, not hollow-wall anchors. We also center the bowl to ADA spacing.

    Setting a customer-supplied standard toilet usually costs $200 to $400 in labor. With a mid-range toilet included, most installs land between $450 and $900. A wall-hung toilet with its carrier runs $1,500 to $3,500 or more, since the wall must be opened and refinished. We quote a flat price.

    Yes, and it is a regular part of remodels. On a wood floor it is fairly simple. On a concrete slab we saw-cut the floor, reroute the 3 inch drain and its vent to the new spot, get the work inspected, and then patch the concrete before any tile goes down. Expect a few days.

    We guarantee the workmanship on every toilet we set. Any leak from our install is our problem. That covers the seal and the supply connection, while manufacturing defects in the toilet fall under the maker's warranty, which we help you file if a part fails early. Hang on to your receipt.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most homes measure 12 inches. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    All the major ones, including Kohler, TOTO, American Standard and Gerber. Feel free to run a model past us first. We can tell you which models have strong flush ratings and which ones use proprietary parts that are hard to find when a flapper or fill valve wears out years from now.

    TOTO and American Standard do well in independent flush tests, and Kohler is close behind, with parts at most supply houses. Gerber and Mansfield are solid on a budget. We match the model to your rough-in and how the bathroom gets used.
