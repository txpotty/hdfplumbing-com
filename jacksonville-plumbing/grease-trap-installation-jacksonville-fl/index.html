    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Install in Jacksonville, FL

    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Jacksonville

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. We size the trap to that peak flow, not the average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. The install covers setting the unit, piping the inlet and outlet, and adding the vented flow control the maker specifies.

    Zurn Grease Trap Installation in Jacksonville

    Zurn's indoor grease interceptors cover the common sizes from small under-sink units up to 100 GPM, and we set enough of them to know where the gaskets give trouble. The lid gasket is the usual leak point after a few years. We keep spares on the truck. We mount the supplied flow control on the inlet, at the height the instructions list, and vent it.

    Food Truck Commissary and Ghost Kitchen Traps in Jacksonville, FL

    Commissary kitchens see a dozen trucks dumping wash water in the same two hours, which is a different load from a restaurant spreading it over a long shift. Sizing has to cover that short, heavy spike. Shared delivery-only kitchens can have more sinks per square foot than any dining room, and the trap must handle all of them. Each brand's prep sink counts.

    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Jacksonville, FL

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. Long runs to the trap let grease cool and coat the line, so we keep the trap as close to the fixtures as we can. Closer is better. For outdoor tanks we measure the hose run from where a pump truck can park.

    Grease Trap Brands We Install in Jacksonville

    Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura cover most of what we set in commercial kitchens, plus precast concrete tanks for the big outdoor interceptors. Each brand has its own lid gasket and baffle layout. Parts availability matters. We prefer models whose lids and gaskets are stocked nearby, because waiting a week on a part means waiting a week to reopen.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Jacksonville

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. The air intake has to be vented to code, not left open in the kitchen. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. A trap that sits out of level loses capacity, so we shim it flat before connecting anything.

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Jacksonville

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. Put a 50 GPM unit on a 75 GPM load and grease goes out the back at every rush. We do the math on paper. The sink volume and drain-down time give the GPM number, and we show you the calculation.

    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Jacksonville, FL

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. We see them in licensed home kitchens and in houses where the sewer lateral keeps clogging with grease. A small 4 to 7 GPM unit under the kitchen sink catches most of it, and cleaning takes about ten minutes with a spatula and a bucket. Check with your local health office first.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans.     As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap.
