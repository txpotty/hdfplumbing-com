    What Goes Into an Ejector System in Jacksonville, FL

    New Construction Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Jacksonville, FL

    On a new build, we set the basin ahead of the slab pour. That is the cheapest time, since there is no concrete to cut, and we stub the drains, the vent and the discharge to the plans so the rough-in inspection goes smoothly. It keeps the pour on schedule.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Jacksonville

    Residential ejector systems in Jacksonville serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Jacksonville

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so the toilet, shower and sink drain to a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. Then we patch the concrete.

    Commercial Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Jacksonville, FL

    Businesses in Jacksonville, FL with restrooms, kitchens or break rooms below grade need ejector systems that keep up with heavier, steadier use than a house. A duplex system with an alternating panel is common, so if one pump fails the other keeps things running. Downtime costs money. We size both pumps to carry the full load.

    Zoeller Ejector Pump Installation in Jacksonville, FL

    Zoeller has made ejector pumps for a very long time, and its cast iron models are the ones we see still running in older basements. We install Zoeller for both new basins and drop-in replacements, and its standard models often fit existing pits and discharge piping without changes. Parts are easy to find.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Jacksonville

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. Sump pumps push groundwater out to the yard, ejectors push sewage to the sewer, and only the ejector pit must be sealed and vented against gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Jacksonville

    An ejector failure usually announces itself with the alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit. Stop all use of the basement fixtures and call, because once a plumber is on site most emergency replacements take just a few hours. Trucks stock popular models.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in Jacksonville

    Ejector pumps usually last 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Signs of trouble include a pump that runs constantly, a grinding or humming noise, an alarm that keeps tripping and sewage odor near the pit, and any of those means it is time to call before the basement floor tells you. Old switches fail first.
