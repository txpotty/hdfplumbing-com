    Disposals in Remodels and New Builds in Jacksonville

    On new builds and remodels in Jacksonville, we coordinate with the general contractor so the disposal, the sink and the dishwasher are planned together. Early planning prevents rework.

    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in Jacksonville

    Every new disposal has a plug sealing the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. A plug left in backs up the dishwasher. We also install an air gap or high loop, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher while the disposal is draining.

    Standard Disposal Installation in Jacksonville

    Most households in Jacksonville do well with a 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower continuous-feed unit. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Then we wire it and test it. We listen for a smooth spin and watch the drain clear.

    Batch-Feed Units for Kitchens Without a Switch in Jacksonville

    These disposals run only with the stopper lid seated, which adds safety for families with kids. Because they need no wall switch, they suit kitchens where a new switch would mean cutting drywall or a tile backsplash. Mounting and drains are standard.

    Disposal Installation for Double Sinks in Jacksonville, FL

    Double bowl sinks put the disposal on one side and a continuous waste or end outlet tee on the other. We lay out the drain so both bowls drain freely and the disposal outlet does not push water into the other bowl, which happens when the tee sits too close to the disposal discharge. We use heavy-wall fittings.

    Disposal Installation on an Existing Sink in Jacksonville

    With a typical 1/2 or 3/4 HP unit, we remove the old unit if there is one and install the new one on the existing sink. We connect it to the drain, set the P-trap and wire it, then check the clearance under the sink, which can be tight in older Jacksonville homes with shallow cabinets or a water filter mounted on the back wall. Tight cabinets need planning. Sometimes a compact model is the better fit.

    Disposal Rough-In for New Construction in Jacksonville

    Rough-in decides where the outlet and drain end up before the cabinets go in. For a kitchen with a disposal, we usually set the drain stub lower than a plain sink would need, so the trap can sit below the disposal outlet with room for proper fall. A switched outlet goes in at this stage. We mark its spot so it clears the trap.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Jacksonville

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. Power needs a check too. A larger motor pulls more current when it starts.
