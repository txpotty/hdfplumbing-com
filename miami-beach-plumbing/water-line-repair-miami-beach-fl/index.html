    Copper Water Line Repair in Miami Beach

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, then we look over the pipe on either side for more pitting before we backfill.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Miami Beach, FL

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, since most service lines past 40 years old are living on borrowed time. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    After-Hours Water Line Breaks in Miami Beach, FL

    Water lines tend to break on a Saturday night. Ours is a 24 hour line.     Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Miami Beach

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Many homes in Florida built before the mid 1970s have cast iron drains under the slab, which commonly crack and corrode with age. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Miami Beach, FL

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patches on those materials rarely hold. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, usually in one day with a trench or a trenchless pull. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Miami Beach, FL

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Many wells in Florida produce water with a sulfur smell or high iron, which often calls for treatment to protect fixtures and appliances. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Miami Beach, FL

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Fittings in the box loosen with ground movement and traffic. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, because the water provider owns the meter and everything on the street side. When it is on your side, we repair it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Miami Beach, FL

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rodents chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.
