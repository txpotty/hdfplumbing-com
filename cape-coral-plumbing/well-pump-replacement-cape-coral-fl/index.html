    Franklin Electric Well Pump Replacement in Cape Coral

    Franklin Electric builds a large share of the submersible motors sold in the US, including ones sitting under other brands' pump ends. Parts and control boxes are easy to find. We match the motor to the pump end and the wire size to the run length.

    Constant Pressure Well Pump Installation in Cape Coral, FL

    Constant pressure systems hold the house at one pressure, say 50 psi, whether one faucet is open or the shower and the irrigation are running together. The VFD changes the motor speed to match how much water you are using. No more pressure dip mid-shower.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Cape Coral, FL

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. Air sneaks in through a pinhole in the suction line, the pump loses prime and the seal burns up running dry. We pressure test the line. A well that is too deep for suction needs a submersible, and we will quote that instead.

    Solar Well Pump Systems in Cape Coral, FL

    Solar well pumps make sense for livestock tanks and cabins where running power from the meter would cost more than the pump itself. A DC pump and a controller run off a few panels during daylight. The storage tank does the job a battery would. Panel count comes from how many gallons you need a day.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Cape Coral

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. Depending on depth and pump weight, it gets replaced with schedule 120 PVC or poly. The wire gets checked too. Nicked jackets and old tape splices go.

    Well Pump Brands We Install in Cape Coral, FL

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Sta-Rite cover nearly every well pump we install. Each brand has strengths, and we choose based on your well. Parts for Goulds and Franklin are easy to get, which matters when a control box dies on a weekend.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Cape Coral

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Sand in the impellers and lightning surges are the usual culprits. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.
