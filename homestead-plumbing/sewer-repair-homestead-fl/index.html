    After-Hours Sewer Repair in Homestead

    Main lines like to fail on holidays. We still answer. An after-hours call gets a tech with a cable machine and a camera, which is enough to restore flow in most cases and to find out whether the trouble is a blockage or a structural break. If it is structural, we schedule the dig for the earliest safe window. We avoid digging blind at night near gas and electric lines.

    Pipe Burst & Collapse Repair in Homestead, FL

    A collapsed sewer pipe stops all flow at the point of failure. The whole house loses its drains. Collapses usually come from corroded cast iron, crushed clay under a driveway or tar-paper pipe that has finally flattened, and the camera tells us exactly where the pipe gave way and how deep it sits. We dig at the mark and replace the broken length with PVC. Lining cannot fix a full collapse.

    Root Intrusion Removal in Homestead, FL

    Roots enter through hairline cracks and loose joints, then grow into a mat that catches paper and grease. Cut roots regrow within a year or two. We clear the line with a root cutting head or a jetter with a chain flail, camera it afterward to see the condition of each joint, and show you whether a liner or a spot repair would stop the cycle for good. Mature trees in Homestead yards make this a common call.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Homestead

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. Crushed pipe cannot be lined. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. Older homes often need this on at least one section. We stage the work so you lose sewer service for a few hours, not days.

    Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Homestead, FL

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Waste collects in the dip. Limestone bedrock and sinkhole activity in some areas can shift the ground and crack buried water and sewer lines. We measure the depth of standing water on camera, dig out that section, re-bed the pipe on compacted gravel and reset the slope to the quarter inch per foot most codes call for on residential laterals. Not every sag needs digging, and we will tell you which yours is.

    Sewer Pipe Relining in Homestead, FL

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line with a jetter first. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, which seals cracks and root entry points in a single continuous tube. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. On a four-inch residential line, that rarely matters.

    Root Intrusion Repair in Homestead, FL

    Cutting roots treats the symptom. The joint is the real problem. After clearing the line, we look at each rooted joint on camera and decide between a short sectional liner, a full liner or cutting in new pipe, depending on how many joints are affected and how far apart they sit. One or two joints is a small job. A line rooted end to end is a lining or replacement job.

    Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in Homestead, FL

    A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Sewer gas follows soon after. Bird nests, leaves and ice can block a roof vent, and older galvanized or cast iron vent stacks crack inside the walls where nobody sees them, which is why we look at the vent whenever the lateral checks out fine. We clear it from the roof or replace the damaged section.

    A single spot repair typically runs $1,500 to $4,500. Deeper pipe costs more to reach.     A spot repair or a jetting job usually takes a few hours. Lining takes a day or two. A full replacement with excavation can take two to five days, depending on the length of the run, the depth of the pipe and how long the inspection takes to schedule. The schedule is in the written estimate.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. Watch the tub. We confirm it on camera.

    For a spot repair, you lose the sewer for a few hours. Replacements take longer. On a full replacement we stage the work so the house is reconnected each evening where possible, and we tell you which fixtures are safe to use while the line is open. Most families stay home through it.

    Clay, cast iron, ABS, PVC and Orangeburg all come up in Homestead and around Florida. We work on all five. Joining new PVC to old clay or cast iron takes a shielded transition coupling sized to the outside diameter of each pipe, and getting that fitting right is what keeps the repair from leaking at the joint later. The usual couplings ride on the truck.
